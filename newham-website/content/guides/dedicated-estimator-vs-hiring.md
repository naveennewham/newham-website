If your M&E tender pipeline is steady and you need estimating capacity soon, a dedicated estimator team gives you people who work only on your projects, without you recruiting or employing them. If the role is core to your business for the long term and you can keep someone busy all year, hiring an estimator in Singapore builds that capability in-house. Many contractors do both: a small in-house commercial team that owns pricing and sign-off, supported by dedicated estimators or quantity surveyors who handle take-offs, BOQs and variation pricing. This guide compares the two routes and shows how to set up quantity surveyor outsourcing so it works.

## What each option involves

### Hiring an estimator in-house

You recruit, employ and manage the person directly. You get someone fully embedded in your company, but you also carry the work around the role:

- Recruitment time, which depends on the market and the seniority you need
- Onboarding to your templates, rate library and tender formats
- HR, payroll, leave cover and performance management
- For foreign hires, meeting the Ministry of Manpower work pass requirements. Check the current rules at [mom.gov.sg](https://www.mom.gov.sg)
- Keeping the person productive in quiet months

### A dedicated outsourced estimator

A provider employs the estimator or QS, who works only on your projects and your hours. You direct the work. The provider handles hiring, training, HR and quality. With Newham, engagements run monthly, are billed in SGD, carry no recruitment or setup fees, and the scope is confirmed in writing before work starts.

### Per-deliverable outsourcing

You send a tender or a claim and receive a defined output. This suits irregular workloads. It is covered in more detail in [outsourcing MEP estimation](/guides/outsourcing-mep-estimation).

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | In-house hire | Dedicated outsourced estimator |
|---|---|---|
| Who employs the person | You | The provider |
| Who directs the work | You | You |
| Recruitment and HR | Your responsibility | Handled by the provider |
| Work pass requirements for foreign hires | Your responsibility | Not your responsibility |
| Adjusting capacity | Hiring or letting go | Monthly, by agreement |
| Learning your templates and rates | Yes, over time | Yes, over time |
| Presence on site and at client meetings | Yes | Limited, depending on the arrangement |
| Best for | Long-term core roles | Steady pipelines and growing teams |

Neither option removes the need for your own commercial judgement. Final prices, margins and the decision to bid stay with you either way. The real difference is who carries the employment work, and how quickly you can change the size of the team when your pipeline changes.

## How to decide

Ask these questions:

- [ ] Do we price tenders every month, or only in bursts?
- [ ] Is the bottleneck measurement and BOQ work, or pricing decisions?
- [ ] Can we keep a full-time estimator productive in quiet months?
- [ ] How soon do we need the capacity?
- [ ] Do we have someone to direct and review the estimator's work?
- [ ] Do we need the person on site or at client meetings regularly?
- [ ] Do we have templates and a rate library to share?

If the bottleneck is take-offs and BOQs, the pipeline is steady and you need help soon, a dedicated estimator usually fits. If you need someone to negotiate with clients and attend site regularly, hire.

## Setting up a dedicated estimator team, step by step

1. **Define the role.** List the tasks: take-offs, BOQ compilation, rate build-up from your prices, variation pricing, progress claims. Note which trades they cover.
2. **Choose the size.** Start with a single specialist, or a small blended team such as an estimator and a drafter, and adjust monthly.
3. **Confirm the scope in writing.** Roles, seniority, hours, deliverables and the review process, agreed before work starts.
4. **Share your standards.** BOQ templates, rate libraries, supplier price lists, tender formats and a past submission you were happy with.
5. **Name one lead.** One person on your side directs the work and answers queries.
6. **Run a pilot tender.** Use a live job with a real deadline and review it as you would a new hire's first tender.
7. **Agree the review step.** Decide who checks quantities, rates and scope before anything is submitted.
8. **Review monthly.** Check output and workload, and add or release capacity as projects start and close.

## What a dedicated estimator can take on

- Material and labour take-offs from tender drawings and specifications
- BOQ compilation in your or the client's format
- Rate build-up from your price lists and supplier quotes
- Tender queries, addenda review and scope gap analysis
- Variation pricing, budget against actual and interim valuations
- Progress claims and supporting documents

## What to keep in-house

A dedicated estimator adds capacity to your commercial process. It does not replace it. Whichever route you choose, keep these with your own team:

- Final prices, margins and the decision to bid
- Client and main contractor relationships and negotiations
- Approval of anything submitted under your company's name
- Commercial positions on variations and claims

Writing this down at the start avoids confusion later about who decides what.

## What the first month looks like

The first weeks are about learning your way of working. Expect the estimator to ask a lot of questions about your templates, rate sources and how you treat items such as preliminaries, provisional sums and exclusions. Answer them in writing where you can, and keep the answers in a shared note, so they become part of your standards rather than one-off replies. Review the first jobs closely, feed back on anything that does not match your practice, and check that the same point does not come up twice. The aim is that, over time, the output needs less correction and your reviewer can focus on pricing judgement instead of formatting.

## Common mistakes

- **Hiring for a peak.** Recruiting a permanent estimator for one busy quarter leaves you with spare capacity later.
- **Outsourcing pricing decisions.** The estimator prepares the numbers. Your team sets margins and decides to bid.
- **No templates or rate library.** Without them, every BOQ comes back in a different shape.
- **Skipping the pilot.** A live tender shows quality faster than any interview.
- **Several people giving instructions.** One lead keeps the work consistent.
- **Changing people often.** A dedicated estimator gets better as they learn your rates. Rotating staff throws that away.
- **Generic offshore staff.** Estimators who do not know Singapore M&E trades and tender formats need more supervision than the work is worth.

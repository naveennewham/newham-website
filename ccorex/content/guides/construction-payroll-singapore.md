Construction workforce management in Singapore comes down to three things: knowing who was on which site and when, staying within MOM's foreign workforce rules, and turning approved attendance into correct pay. A phone-based foreign worker time attendance app with site geolocation handles the first part without kiosk hardware, and linking approved attendance to payroll removes most of the month-end reconciliation.

This guide covers site attendance options, the MOM rules that shape your workforce, and how to run construction payroll with fewer errors.

## The workforce rules that shape construction payroll

Construction firms in Singapore rely heavily on foreign workers on Work Permits. MOM's [construction sector requirements page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/sector-specific-rules/construction-sector-requirements) sets the main rules:

| Rule | What MOM sets |
|---|---|
| Dependency ratio ceiling | 5 Work Permit holders for each local worker earning the qualifying salary |
| Higher-skilled share | At least 10% of Work Permit holders must be higher-skilled (R1) |
| Foreign worker levy | Charged per worker per month, ranging from S$300 to S$900 depending on skill and source |

Two practical consequences for payroll and HR:

- **Your local headcount matters.** The number of Work Permit holders you can employ depends on local workers earning the qualifying salary, so local payroll records feed directly into workforce planning.
- **Skill level affects cost.** Because the levy varies by skill and source, keeping skill records accurate is a cost issue, not just an HR one.

Check MOM's page for current details, including how the qualifying salary is defined, before making hiring decisions.

## Statutory contributions: CPF, SDL and SHG

For employees who are Singapore citizens or permanent residents, employers make CPF contributions, with rates that depend on the employee's age and wages. Rates change from time to time, so always use the current tables from the [CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/).

The Skills Development Levy (SDL) is payable for employees, including foreign workers, and is administered by SkillsFuture Singapore. See [skillsfuture.gov.sg](https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/) for the current rules.

Self-help group (SHG) fund contributions are deducted for eligible employees based on their community, unless they opt out.

Your payroll software should apply these from current tables, not from rates someone typed into a spreadsheet years ago.

## Site attendance options compared

Accurate attendance is the foundation of construction payroll. These are the common approaches:

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Paper sign-in sheets | Cheap, no setup | Buddy-punching, illegible entries, manual typing into payroll |
| Kiosk or fingerprint device at the gate | Harder to fake | Hardware to buy and maintain at each site, breaks down, needs power and network |
| App on a supervisor's phone | No extra hardware | Relies on supervisor being present and honest |
| App on each worker's own phone with face and GPS | No kiosk hardware, identity and location captured | Workers need a smartphone, needs offline handling |

### What to look for in a foreign worker time attendance app

- [ ] Face check to confirm the right person is punching in
- [ ] GPS location so you can see the punch happened at the site
- [ ] Works on the worker's own phone, no shared device
- [ ] Works without signal by storing punches and uploading later
- [ ] Simple interface for workers with limited English
- [ ] HR or supervisor approval of each session before it reaches payroll
- [ ] Attendance linked to the project or site for costing

## Site attendance geolocation: how it works in practice

With site attendance geolocation, each site has a known location. When a worker clocks in, the app records the time, a face image or check, and the phone's GPS position. HR can then see where each punch took place.

Points to plan for:

- **Basements and enclosed areas.** GPS can be weak indoors and signal may drop. Offline queuing ensures the punch is not lost.
- **Multiple sites in one day.** Workers who move between sites need separate sessions, each recorded against the right project.
- **Exceptions.** Missed punches will happen. A clear approval step lets HR correct them with a record of why.

## From attendance to payroll

Once attendance is approved, payroll should take it directly. A clean monthly process looks like this:

1. Workers clock in and out on their phones at each site.
2. Supervisors or HR review and approve each session.
3. Leave applications are approved in the same system.
4. Approved attendance and leave flow into payroll.
5. Payroll applies overtime, allowances and deductions.
6. Statutory contributions (CPF, SDL, SHG funds) are calculated from current rules.
7. Payslips are issued and payroll is posted.
8. Labour hours are allocated to projects for job costing.

The last step matters for margin. Labour is often the largest cost a project team cannot see. Our guide to [budget vs actual in construction](/resources/budget-vs-actual-construction) explains how labour feeds project cost.

## Rolling out phone attendance on site

Changing how workers clock in affects everyone on site, so plan the rollout.

1. **Start with one site.** Pick a site with a supportive supervisor and iron out problems there first.
2. **Register sites properly.** Set each site's location and check it on the ground, including basements and lay-down areas.
3. **Enrol workers in person.** Register faces and install the app with the supervisor present. Explain in simple terms, and in workers' own languages where possible, what is recorded and why.
4. **Run alongside the old method briefly.** Compare the new records with paper or kiosk records for a short period, then switch off the old method.
5. **Set the approval routine.** Decide who approves sessions and how often, so payroll is never waiting on a backlog.
6. **Handle personal data carefully.** Face images and locations are personal data. Tell workers how they are used and restrict access to those who need it.

Expect some questions in the first weeks: workers who change phones, punches made from the site office car park, and supervisors who forget to approve. Treat these as setup issues, fix the underlying cause, and the routine settles quickly once everyone sees that pay follows approved attendance.

## Records worth keeping

Good records protect you when there is a dispute or an inspection. Keep, for each worker:

- Approved attendance by date, site and project
- Leave applications and approvals
- Overtime and allowance calculations
- Payslips and statutory contribution records
- Skill level and permit details used for workforce planning

## Common mistakes

- **Typing paper attendance into payroll.** Every manual step adds errors and delays.
- **Buying kiosks for every site.** Hardware breaks, gets moved and needs maintenance. Workers' own phones avoid most of this.
- **No offline handling.** Punches lost in a basement become disputes on payday.
- **Skipping approval.** Unapproved attendance going straight to payroll pays for mistakes.
- **Out-of-date statutory rates.** CPF and other contributions change; check the official sources each time rules are updated.
- **Ignoring the dependency ratio when hiring locals.** Local payroll records affect how many Work Permit holders you can keep.
- **Not costing labour to projects.** Payroll is correct, but project margins are wrong.

To calculate overtime pay in Singapore, take the employee’s **hourly basic rate of pay**, multiply it by **1.5**, then by the number of **overtime hours** worked in the salary period. For a monthly-rated employee, MOM’s hourly basic rate is **(12 × monthly basic salary) ÷ (52 × 44)**. These rules apply to employees covered by Part 4 of the Employment Act: non-workmen earning a basic salary of $2,600 or less a month, and workmen earning $4,500 or less.

This guide sets out who is covered, what counts as normal hours, the formula with worked examples, and the separate rules for rest days and public holidays. Figures are as published by the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) in October 2026, mainly on its [hours of work, overtime and rest days](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/hours-of-work-overtime-and-rest-days) page; check it before you rely on them.

## Who is entitled to overtime pay

Overtime rules sit in **Part 4 of the Employment Act**, which regulates hours of work. As of October 2026, MOM says Part 4 covers:

- **Non-workmen** earning a monthly basic salary of **$2,600 or less**.
- **Workmen** earning a monthly basic salary of **$4,500 or less**.

MOM’s website explains who counts as a workman. Part 4 does **not** cover managers and executives. For employees outside Part 4, overtime pay (if any) follows the employment contract.

The test uses the **basic** salary, not gross pay, so an employee with a low basic salary and large allowances can still be covered.

## Normal hours of work

Overtime is all work beyond the employee’s normal hours, excluding breaks. MOM sets the normal hours limits for Part 4 employees:

| Work pattern | Normal hours |
|---|---|
| 5 days or fewer a week | Up to 9 hours a day or 44 hours a week |
| More than 5 days a week | Up to 8 hours a day or 44 hours a week |
| Alternate-week arrangements | Up to 48 hours a week, no more than 88 hours in any continuous 2-week period |
| Shift work | Up to 12 hours a day, averaging 44 hours over 3 continuous weeks |

Write the normal hours into the contract and the work schedule. Every hour past them is an overtime hour.

## The overtime formula

MOM’s formula is:

**Overtime pay = hourly basic rate of pay × 1.5 × overtime hours**

The rate is **at least** 1.5 times the hourly basic rate. An employer can pay more, but not less.

### Working out the hourly basic rate

| How the employee is paid | Hourly basic rate of pay |
|---|---|
| Monthly-rated | (12 × monthly basic rate of pay) ÷ (52 × 44) |
| Daily-rated | Daily basic pay ÷ working hours per day |
| Piece-rated | Total weekly pay at the basic rate ÷ total hours worked that week |

### What counts as basic pay

MOM’s [salary definitions](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/salary/monthly-and-daily-salary) separate the two rates used in payroll:

- **Basic rate of pay** includes the wage adjustments and increments the employee is entitled to under the contract. It **excludes** overtime, bonuses, the annual wage supplement (AWS), reimbursement of special work expenses, productivity incentives and **any allowance**.
- **Gross rate of pay** includes the allowances in the contract. It excludes overtime, bonuses, AWS, special expense reimbursement, productivity incentives, and travel, food and housing allowances.

Overtime uses the **basic** rate. Gross pay comes in elsewhere, for example for holiday pay when a public holiday falls on a non-working day.

### The non-workman cap

For non-workmen, MOM caps the overtime rate at the **$2,600 salary level, which is an hourly basic rate of $13.60**. Even if the formula gives a higher hourly rate, overtime is paid at 1.5 × $13.60.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Workman, monthly basic salary $2,200, 10 overtime hours.**

- Hourly basic rate: (12 × $2,200) ÷ (52 × 44) = $26,400 ÷ 2,288 = $11.538…, rounded to **$11.54**
- Overtime rate: $11.54 × 1.5 = $17.31 an hour
- Overtime pay: $17.31 × 10 = **$173.10**

Any site or transport allowance is left out, because allowances are not part of the basic rate.

**Example 2: Non-workman, monthly basic salary $2,600, 8 overtime hours.**

- The formula gives (12 × $2,600) ÷ 2,288 = $13.636…, which is above the cap.
- Capped hourly basic rate: **$13.60**
- Overtime rate: $13.60 × 1.5 = $20.40 an hour
- Overtime pay: $20.40 × 8 = **$163.20**

**Example 3: The workman from Example 1 works on a rest day at the employer’s request.**

The normal working day is 9 hours.

- The workman works 6 hours, which is more than half the normal daily hours, and is owed **2 days’ salary** at the basic rate.
- For 11 hours instead, the workman would be owed 2 days’ salary plus 2 hours of overtime: $11.54 × 1.5 × 2 = **$34.62** on top. Those 2 hours count toward the 72-hour monthly limit.

Use MOM’s rest day pay calculator to work out the day’s salary for a monthly-rated employee.

## Rest days

Part 4 employees are entitled to **1 rest day a week**: a whole day from midnight to midnight. It is **not a paid day**. An employer cannot make an employee work on a rest day except in exceptional circumstances.

Pay for working on a rest day depends on who asked for the work and how long it lasted:

| Hours worked on the rest day | At the employer’s request | At the employee’s request |
|---|---|---|
| Up to half the normal daily hours | 1 day’s salary | Half a day’s salary |
| More than half the normal daily hours | 2 days’ salary | 1 day’s salary |
| Beyond the normal daily hours | 2 days’ salary + overtime pay | 1 day’s salary + overtime pay |

For work beyond the normal daily hours, MOM’s formula is:

**(hourly basic rate × 1.5 × overtime hours) + rest day or public holiday pay**

## Public holidays

Employees covered by the Employment Act are entitled to **11 paid public holidays a year**. MOM’s [public holiday rules](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays-entitlement-and-pay) set out what happens when they work, or when the holiday falls on a day off:

- **Working on a public holiday:** an **extra day’s salary at the basic rate**. The monthly salary already covers the holiday itself. By agreement, the employer can give another public holiday in lieu instead.
- **Time off in lieu** is an option only for employees **not** covered by Part 4. Where no duration is agreed, MOM allows 4 hours off for 4 hours of work or less, or a full day off for longer.
- **Public holiday on a non-working day** (such as Saturday on a 5-day week): another day off, or one extra day’s salary at the **gross** rate.
- **Public holiday on a rest day:** the next working day becomes a paid public holiday.

There is no holiday pay if the employee was absent without consent or a reasonable excuse on the working day just before or after the holiday, or if the holiday falls within approved unpaid leave.

Hours worked beyond the normal daily hours on a public holiday are paid as overtime and count toward the 72-hour limit.

## The 72-hour cap and the 12-hour day

MOM sets two limits that payroll and site supervisors should both watch:

- **No more than 72 overtime hours a month.** Rest-day and public-holiday work counts only for the hours beyond the normal daily hours.
- **No more than 12 hours of work a day.** There are exceptions in limited circumstances, and work of up to 14 hours a day needs an overtime exemption from MOM.

## Payment deadline and payslips

Overtime pay must be paid **within 14 days after the last day of the salary period**.

Show overtime clearly on the itemised payslip: the overtime hours worked and the overtime pay for the period, separate from basic salary and allowances. This lets an employee check the figure against their own hours, and it keeps your records ready if MOM asks.

## Common mistakes

- **Using gross pay instead of basic pay.** Adding allowances to the hourly rate overpays overtime and makes it inconsistent between employees.
- **Forgetting the non-workman cap.** Non-workmen on $2,600 are paid overtime at 1.5 × $13.60, not at their own formula rate.
- **Counting breaks as overtime,** or forgetting that overtime starts after the normal hours set in the contract.
- **Paying overtime late.** It is due within 14 days after the salary period, not with the following month’s salary if that is later.
- **Missing rest-day and public-holiday extra hours** when checking the 72-hour monthly limit.
- **Paying overtime from timesheets nobody approved.** Disputes start when the hours paid do not match the hours the supervisor signed off.

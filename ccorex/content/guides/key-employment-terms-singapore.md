**Key employment terms (KETs)** are the main terms of a job, given to the employee in writing. In Singapore, employers must issue them to employees covered by the Employment Act whose contract is for **14 days or more**, and they must be issued **within 14 days of the start of employment**. MOM lists **18 items**, from the employer’s name and the job title to salary, leave, probation and notice. A contract that already contains all of them is enough.

This guide covers who must get KETs, the deadline, the 18 items, the formats MOM accepts, and how KETs fit with the employment contract and payroll. As of October 2026, the rules are those on MOM’s [key employment terms](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/contract-of-service/key-employment-terms) page and its [KETs FAQs](https://www.mom.gov.sg/faq/key-employment-terms). Check them before you rely on them.

## Who must get KETs

As of October 2026, MOM says written KETs are required for employees who meet all three conditions:

1. They are **covered by the Employment Act**.
2. They were **employed on or after 1 April 2016**.
3. Their contract is for **14 days or more**. MOM says this is about the length of the contract, not how many days the person works.

Part-time employees who meet these conditions must get KETs too.

## When to issue them

KETs must be issued **within 14 days after the start of employment**. MOM’s example: an employee who starts on 1 January must have them by 15 January. MOM explains that the 14 days give the employer time to put all the terms in writing.

MOM’s tripartite guidelines encourage employers to give KETs **before** work starts where possible. If the terms change later, agree the change with the employee first, then give them the updated terms in writing.

## The 18 items MOM lists

KETs must include these items, **unless an item doesn’t apply** to the employee:

| # | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full name of employer | |
| 2 | Full name of employee | |
| 3 | Job title, main duties and responsibilities | |
| 4 | Start date of employment | |
| 5 | Duration of employment | Fixed-term contracts only |
| 6 | Working arrangements | Daily working hours, working days a week, and the rest day |
| 7 | Salary period | |
| 8 | Basic salary | Hourly, daily or piece-rated employees also need the basic rate of pay |
| 9 | Fixed allowances | |
| 10 | Fixed deductions | |
| 11 | Overtime payment period | Only if different from the salary period |
| 12 | Overtime rate of pay | |
| 13 | Other salary-related components | Such as bonuses and incentives |
| 14 | Types of leave | Such as annual, outpatient sick, hospitalisation, maternity and childcare leave |
| 15 | Other medical benefits | Such as insurance, medical and dental benefits |
| 16 | Probation period | |
| 17 | Notice period | |
| 18 | Place of work | Optional, but MOM strongly encourages it, especially if it differs from the employer’s address |

For a professional, manager or executive who isn’t paid overtime, items 11 and 12 can be left out.

MOM publishes a blank KETs sample form, a version with explanations, and a KETs verification tool that checks whether the working arrangements meet the Employment Act and can generate the working arrangements and salary sections.

## Formats MOM accepts

Written KETs can be in **soft or hard copy**, including handwritten, and can be given through:

- The **employment contract** or offer letter.
- A **staff handbook**, company website or circulars, for terms common to a group of employees, such as leave or medical benefits.

Whatever the format, the employee should be able to read the terms and keep a copy.

## KETs and the employment contract

KETs aren’t a separate contract. They are the terms an employee must be told in writing, and most employers put them in the contract or offer letter:

| Situation | What MOM says to do |
|---|---|
| The written contract has every required KET | Nothing more; no separate document is needed |
| The contract is missing some KETs | Add them to the contract |
| Some terms sit in the handbook | Fine, as long as the employee can access and keep them |

MOM says employees don’t have to sign their KETs, but it is in the employer’s interest to get an acknowledgement, because it helps prevent disputes.

## Why KETs matter for payroll

Several KETs drive the pay run directly: the **salary period**, **basic salary**, **fixed allowances and deductions**, the **overtime period and rate**, the **working days and rest day**, and the **leave types**. If the payslip and the KETs disagree, the employee will query it. Keep each employee’s KETs and their payroll set-up in step, and update both when a term changes.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until after probation.** KETs are due within 14 days of the start date, probation or not.
- **Leaving out part-time or short-term staff.** Anyone covered by the Employment Act on a contract of 14 days or more needs them.
- **No rest day or working hours.** Item 6 is often missing, and it decides rest-day and overtime pay.
- **Allowances written as “subject to company policy”.** Fixed allowances and deductions should be stated.
- **Changing terms verbally.** Agree changes with the employee and give the updated terms in writing.
- **Payroll set up differently from the KETs.** The salary period, overtime rate and allowances in payroll should match what the employee was given.

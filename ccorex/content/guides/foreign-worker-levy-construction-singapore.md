The foreign worker levy is one of the largest costs a Singapore construction employer controls directly. It is charged every month for every Work Permit holder, and the rate depends on the worker's source country and whether they are Higher-Skilled (R1) or Basic-Skilled (R2). The number of Work Permit holders you can hold at all is capped by a dependency ratio tied to your local workforce, and each permit has an expiry date that must be renewed on time.

This guide explains the levy tiers, the construction work permit quota, what happened to the Man-Year Entitlement (MYE), the routes to higher-skilled R1 status, and Work Permit expiry and renewal. Rules and rates change; check MOM's website for the current position before you act.

## Foreign worker levy rates for construction

MOM sets construction levy rates on its [construction sector requirements page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/sector-specific-rules/construction-sector-requirements). As of October 2026, the page lists these monthly rates, effective from 1 January 2024:

| Worker source | Higher-Skilled (R1) | Basic-Skilled (R2) |
|---|---|---|
| Non-traditional sources (NTS) | S$500 | S$900 |
| Malaysia, North Asian sources (NAS), PRC | S$300 | S$700 |
| Off-site construction | S$250 | S$370 |

A few points from the same page that affect your monthly bill:

- **Workers without the required certification** are treated as unskilled and charged S$900 regardless of source.
- **Part months** are charged at a daily rate for workers who did not work a full calendar month. MOM works it out as the monthly rate × 12 ÷ 365, rounded up to the nearest cent: for example S$9.87 a day for a S$300 R1 worker and S$29.59 a day for a S$900 worker.
- **Skill changes take effect on the 1st of the following month.** Upgrading a worker to R1 on the 3rd does not reduce this month's levy.
- **Levy bond.** MOM lists a S$600 levy bond for each higher-skilled or basic-skilled worker and S$2,000 for each unskilled worker, bought separately for skilled and unskilled workers. The levy bond monitoring period is usually 12 months (6 months for new companies), and any late levy payment during that period extends it by 12 months. This is separate from the security bond, which is only required for non-Malaysian workers.

## Security bond for construction Work Permit holders

MOM's [security bond page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/sector-specific-rules/security-bond) sets out the rules. As of October 2026:

- **S$5,000 per non-Malaysian Work Permit holder.** Malaysian workers do not need one.
- **A banker's or insurer's guarantee.** You buy it from a bank or insurance company, and you cannot ask the worker to pay for it.
- **It must be in place before the worker arrives.** If it has not taken effect, the immigration officer will not let the worker enter Singapore. Allow up to 3 working days for the bank or insurer to send the details to MOM.
- **It can be forfeited** for breaches such as violating Work Permit or bond conditions, paying salary late, or failing to send a worker home when the permit ends.
- **It is discharged** once the permit is cancelled, the worker has gone home and no conditions were breached, usually one week after the worker leaves Singapore.

The bond's expiry date also limits how long a renewed Work Permit can run (see renewal below), so track it alongside the permit.

The gap between R1 and R2 is S$400 a month per worker for both NTS and Malaysia/NAS/PRC workers on site. Across a workforce of 50, that difference adds up quickly, which is why skill tier is a cost decision, not just an HR record.

## The construction work permit quota

Construction uses a dependency ratio ceiling (DRC), which MOM also calls the quota. As of October 2026, MOM's construction page states that you can employ **5 Work Permit holders for each local employee earning the Local Qualifying Salary (LQS)**.

Two practical consequences:

- **Your local headcount sets your foreign headcount.** If a local employee leaves, or their pay falls below the LQS, your quota shrinks. That can block new applications or renewals.
- **Quota is calculated on locals who qualify, not on everyone on payroll.** A part-time or low-paid local employee may not count in full. Check how MOM counts local employees for quota purposes on its LQS guidance before you plan hiring.

### The 10% R1 requirement

On top of the DRC, at least **10% of your construction Work Permit holders must be Higher-Skilled (R1)** before you can hire new R2 workers or renew existing R2 permits. MOM states that permits for excess R2 workers will be revoked. You can check your R1 percentage on WP Online.

This rule catches firms that let R1 workers leave without replacing them. Your total quota may have room, but if your R1 share drops below 10%, R2 renewals stop.

## What happened to the Man-Year Entitlement (MYE)

For many years, main contractors needed MYEs, an allocation based on the value of their construction projects, to hire workers from non-traditional sources and the PRC. Levy rates also differed depending on whether a worker was under an MYE or an MYE waiver.

MOM announced in [February 2022](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2022/0218-supporting-transformation-in-the-process-and-construction-sectors) that the MYE framework for construction would be removed from 1 January 2024. From that date:

- Firms no longer apply for MYEs based on project value.
- The DRC for construction moved to 1:5, the ratio on MOM's page today.
- Levy rates were reset to the source-and-skill structure in the table above.

If your HR records, budget templates or tender assumptions still refer to MYE or MYE-waiver rates, they are out of date. Older spreadsheets are a common source of wrong levy forecasts.

## Routes to higher-skilled R1 status

MOM's [higher-skilled workers for construction page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/sector-specific-rules/higher-skilled-workers-for-construction-sector) lists four ways to upgrade a construction worker from R2 to R1. As of October 2026:

| Route | Experience | Skills | Minimum fixed monthly salary |
|---|---|---|---|
| CoreTrade | At least 4 years in the sector | Pass a CoreTrade skills assessment | None |
| Multi-Skilling Scheme | 4 or 6 years | SEC(K) in a second trade (4 years), or specified safety or other credentials (6 years) | None |
| Direct R1 Pathway | No minimum | Higher-skill SEC(K) for PRC and NTS workers; wider options for Malaysian workers | S$1,600 |
| Market-Based Skills Recognition Framework (MBF) | At least 6 years | No skills test | S$1,600 |

Things employers often miss:

- **R1 status expires.** MOM states that a worker's R1 eligibility is valid for 2 years. Renewal runs through BCA, so track it alongside the permit itself.
- **Salary routes depend on payroll.** For Direct R1 and MBF, the worker's fixed monthly salary is updated in WP Online. If payroll later drops the fixed salary below the threshold, the basis for R1 is gone.
- **The new rate starts the following month.** Plan upgrades so they complete before the month you want the saving.

BCA runs CoreTrade registration and the [Direct R1 Pathway](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/buildsg/manpower/direct-r1-pathway); check BCA's pages for testing centres and current procedures.

## Work Permit validity, expiry and renewal

MOM's [renew a Work Permit page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/renew-a-work-permit) sets out the renewal rules. As of October 2026:

- A renewed permit is **usually valid for 2 years**, but can be shorter.
- **Passport validity** limits it. For a full 2-year permit, the passport needs at least 25 months of validity; otherwise the permit ends 1 month before the passport expires.
- **Security bond validity** limits it. If the bond has less than 26 months left, the permit ends 2 months before the bond expires.
- **Renew 7 to 12 weeks before expiry.**
- **Late renewal costs money.** You are still liable for the levy after the permit expires, and an overstaying fine or levy penalties may apply.

MOM's construction page adds sector conditions that affect renewal: the worker's construction safety certificate must be valid for more than 1 month on the renewal date, the firm must be registered in BCA's Contractors Registration System, and R2 renewals depend on meeting the 10% R1 requirement. MOM also states that Work Permit holders can work until 64.

The simplest way to avoid expiry problems is to treat passport, bond, safety certificate and R1 status as four separate expiry dates, each with its own alert.

## What to track every month

A monthly review takes less than an hour if the data is in one place. For each worker, and in total:

| What to track | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Levy cost per worker | Confirms each worker is billed at the right tier and source rate |
| Levy cost per project | Shows the true labour cost on each job, not just wages |
| Skill tier (R1 or R2) and R1 expiry | Drives the levy rate and the 10% R1 requirement |
| Work Permit expiry | Flags renewals due in the next 7 to 12 weeks |
| Passport, bond and safety certificate expiry | Each can shorten or block a renewal |
| Quota headroom | Locals earning the LQS × 5, minus current Work Permit holders |
| R1 share | Must stay at 10% or more for R2 renewals and new hires |

Two cost views are worth separating. **Levy per worker** catches billing errors and identifies R2 workers who are close to qualifying for R1. **Levy per project** puts the levy into job costing, so project margins reflect the full cost of labour. ## Common mistakes

- **Using MYE-era rates.** The MYE framework was removed from 1 January 2024. Budgets built on old levy tables are wrong.
- **Letting R1 status lapse.** R1 eligibility lasts 2 years. A lapsed worker moves to the R2 rate and may push your R1 share below 10%.
- **Forgetting that local turnover changes quota.** Losing one qualifying local employee can remove room for five Work Permit holders.
- **Renewing too late.** Levy keeps running after expiry, and an overstaying fine may follow.
- **Renewing before the passport or bond.** The permit is cut short to match whichever expires first.
- **Upgrading at the end of the month.** The lower levy only starts on the 1st of the following month.
- **Leaving levy out of project costs.** Wages are allocated to projects, but levy sits in overheads, so job margins look better than they are.
- **Keeping permit data in one person's spreadsheet.** When that person is on leave, renewals are missed.

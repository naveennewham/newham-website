To prepare a BOQ, you break the scope into measurable items, take off a quantity for each one, price each line from a reliable rate source, and add preliminaries, markups and tax in a summary. For MEP and fire protection work the hard part is not the arithmetic but consistency: the same item described, measured and priced the same way every time. This guide walks through the process step by step, with a column structure and checklists you can use on your next tender.

## What a BOQ is for

A bill of quantities (BOQ) is the list of everything that has to be supplied and installed, with a unit and quantity for each item. It does three jobs:

- **Pricing.** Every contractor prices the same list, so the client can compare tenders line by line.
- **Valuation.** During the project, progress claims and variations are measured against BOQ items and rates.
- **Control.** Your own team uses the BOQ as the baseline for purchasing and for tracking cost against budget.

If your BOQ is unclear at tender stage, all three jobs get harder later. A vague description becomes a dispute at variation time; a missing item becomes a cost you absorb.

## BOQ vs Design & Build tenders

How you prepare the BOQ depends on the tender type:

- **BOQ tenders.** The consultant issues the BOQ. Your job is to check it against the drawings, price it and qualify anything that is missing or wrong.
- **Design & Build tenders.** You receive drawings, a specification and performance requirements. You design (or develop the design), measure the quantities yourself and produce your own BOQ.
- **Hybrid tenders.** Part of the scope is billed, part is provisional or left for you to measure. These need the most care because gaps sit between the two halves.

Whichever type you have, the steps below still apply. On a BOQ tender you simply start with a list already drafted for you.

## Step-by-step: how to prepare a BOQ

### 1. Read the tender documents first

Before measuring anything, read the specification, preliminaries, conditions of contract, drawings list and any addenda. Note:

1. The scope boundaries between trades (for example, who supplies power to fire pumps or ACMV equipment).
2. Approved brands or "or equivalent" requirements.
3. Testing, commissioning and authority submission obligations.
4. Method of measurement, if one is specified.
5. Submission format and deadline.

A tender summary that lists these points in one place saves time when you later ask, "Was that in scope?"

### 2. Set up the structure

Decide how the BOQ will be broken down before you start taking off. A common structure for MEP is:

- **Section** by trade or system (Fire Protection, ACMV, ELV, A&A works).
- **Sub-section** by building, block or floor.
- **Item group** by type of work (pipework, fittings, equipment, valves, cabling, devices).

Breaking the work down by building and floor makes it easier to check quantities against drawings and to value progress later.

### 3. Build an area schedule

List the areas you will measure: building, floor and room or zone. For fire protection, record the hazard classification of each area, because it drives sprinkler type, spacing and pipe sizing. For ACMV, note the room use and any special conditions. This schedule becomes the backbone that every quantity hangs from.

### 4. Take off quantities

Measure each item from the drawings: lengths of pipe, duct and cable; counts of heads, diffusers, detectors and devices; numbers of equipment items. Work area by area so nothing is skipped. The [quantity takeoff methods guide](/resources/quantity-takeoff-methods) compares manual, digital and AI-assisted approaches.

### 5. Write clear item descriptions

Each description should allow a reader to price the item without guessing. Include material, size, rating, finish, installation method and anything that affects cost (height, concealed or exposed, supports included or not).

### 6. Price each line

Price from a maintained rate source rather than from memory or an old tender. For MEP, split the rate into:

- **Material price**: supply cost from your supplier quotes or price list.
- **Installation price**: labour, small tools and consumables to fix the item.

The line amount is then quantity × (material price + installation price). Keeping the two parts separate lets you apply different markups and check each against market prices.

### 7. Add markups, preliminaries and provisional sums

Apply your material and installation markups, then add preliminaries (site management, insurances, temporary works, testing and commissioning, authority submissions) as the tender requires. Show provisional sums and prime cost items exactly as instructed.

### 8. Build the summary

Roll up sections and sub-sections into a summary page. Show the subtotal before tax, GST at the prevailing rate (9% in Singapore at the time of writing) and the grand total, unless the tender asks for a different layout.

### 9. Check and approve

Have someone other than the estimator review the BOQ before submission. Use the checklist later in this guide.

## A practical BOQ column structure

This structure works for most MEP and fire protection BOQs. It keeps the client-facing columns simple while preserving the detail you need internally.

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Item no. | Unique reference, e.g. FP-2-05 |
| Description | What is supplied and installed, with size and spec |
| Location | Building / floor / area |
| Unit | m, nr, set, lot, item |
| Quantity | Measured quantity |
| Material rate | Supply cost per unit |
| Installation rate | Labour cost per unit |
| Unit rate | Material + installation |
| Amount | Quantity × unit rate |
| Remarks | Brand, assumptions, exclusions |

If the client's BOQ only has "rate" and "amount", keep your detailed version internally and carry the combined rate onto their form.

## Units of measurement for common MEP items

Use consistent units so quantities can be checked and compared:

- **Pipes, ducts, cable trays, cables:** linear metres (m)
- **Sprinkler heads, detectors, diffusers, outlets:** number (nr)
- **Pumps, AHUs, panels, chillers:** number or set
- **Insulation:** linear metres for pipework; square metres for ductwork, depending on the method of measurement
- **Testing and commissioning:** item or lot

State in your remarks whether fittings, supports and accessories are included in the pipe rate or measured separately. Either is acceptable; mixing both in the same BOQ is not.

## Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Every drawing in the drawings list has been reviewed
- [ ] All addenda and tender clarifications are reflected
- [ ] Each area in the area schedule has quantities against it
- [ ] Descriptions state size, material and specification
- [ ] Units are consistent across similar items
- [ ] Rates come from current supplier prices, not an old tender
- [ ] Material and installation markups applied as approved
- [ ] Preliminaries, testing and commissioning included
- [ ] Provisional sums and PC items shown as instructed
- [ ] GST shown separately at the prevailing rate
- [ ] Exclusions and qualifications listed
- [ ] Arithmetic checked: amounts, subtotals and summary

## Common mistakes

1. **Pricing the client's BOQ without checking it.** A billed quantity can be wrong or an item missing. Check against drawings and qualify differences in writing.
2. **Copying rates from an old project.** Material prices move. If a rate has no date and no source, you cannot defend it.
3. **Mixing units or measurement rules.** Measuring fittings separately in one section and including them in another leads to double counting or gaps.
4. **Vague descriptions.** "Supply and install piping" cannot be priced or valued. State size, material, jointing and support.
5. **Forgetting non-material items.** Testing, commissioning, authority submissions, as-built drawings and warranty obligations cost money.
6. **One person, no review.** A second pair of eyes catches transposed numbers and missed areas.
7. **Breaking the client's format.** Adding or renumbering items on the client's BOQ makes your tender hard to compare and may count against you.

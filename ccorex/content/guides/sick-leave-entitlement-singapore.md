In Singapore, an employee covered by the Employment Act who has worked for **at least 3 months** gets paid sick leave. From **6 months of service**, that is **14 days of outpatient sick leave** and up to **60 days of hospitalisation leave** a year, and the 60 days include the 14. To qualify, the employee needs a valid medical certificate (MC) and must inform the employer **within 48 hours** of being absent.

This guide sets out who qualifies, the entitlement by months of service, what makes an MC valid, and how sick leave sits alongside annual leave and payroll. As of October 2026, the figures are those MOM publishes on its [sick leave eligibility and entitlement](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave/sick-leave/eligibility-and-entitlement) page. Check it before you rely on them.

## Who is entitled to paid sick leave

MOM sets three conditions. The employee must:

1. Be **covered by the Employment Act**.
2. Have served the employer for **at least 3 months**.
3. **Inform, or try to inform, the employer within 48 hours** of the absence.

The employee must also be certified unfit for work by a qualifying doctor or dentist (see the MC rules below). Sick leave taken in the first 3 months of service is unpaid. Employees not covered by the Employment Act get sick leave on the terms in their contract.

## Sick leave days by months of service

As of October 2026, MOM says paid sick leave builds up over the first 6 months, then stays at the full entitlement for each year of service:

| Months of service | Paid outpatient sick leave (days) | Paid hospitalisation leave (days) |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer than 3 | None (unpaid) | None (unpaid) |
| 3 | 5 | 15 |
| 4 | 8 | 30 |
| 5 | 11 | 45 |
| 6 and after | 14 | 60 |

### Hospitalisation leave includes outpatient leave

The two numbers are not added together. The 60 days of hospitalisation leave **include** the 14 days of outpatient sick leave. So an employee with 6 months of service who has already taken 14 outpatient days that year can take up to 46 more days of hospitalisation leave, not 60.

## What makes an MC valid

The certificate needed depends on the type of sick leave:

| Type of sick leave | Who must certify the employee |
|---|---|
| Outpatient sick leave | A medical practitioner registered under the Medical Registration Act, or a dentist registered under the Dental Registration Act |
| Hospitalisation leave | The employee is warded or has day surgery, is quarantined under written law, or is certified by a practitioner who can admit patients to hospital (MOM also lists certain national specialty centre and ambulatory surgical centre practitioners) |

MOM advises employees to tell the employer as soon as possible and to hand in the MC when they return to work. Write down how MCs should be submitted in your leave policy, so employees know what to send and to whom.

## Sick leave and annual leave

Sick leave and annual leave are **separate entitlements**. An employee on a valid MC who meets the conditions is on paid sick leave, and their annual leave balance is not touched. As of October 2026, MOM sets annual leave at 7 days in the first year of service, rising by one day a year to 14 days. See our [leave entitlement guide](/resources/leave-entitlement-singapore) for the full table.

| | Outpatient sick leave | Annual leave |
|---|---|---|
| Starts after | 3 months of service | 3 months of service |
| Full entitlement | 14 days a year from 6 months | 7 days in year 1, rising to 14 days from year 8 |
| Needs an MC | Yes | No |
| Planned in advance | No | Yes, with the employer’s approval |

Employers can give more sick leave than the Employment Act requires. Many contracts do. The Act sets the minimum.

## How sick leave affects payroll

Paid sick leave is a paid day: the employee’s salary for the period is not reduced for it. Payroll needs two things from the leave record:

- **Whether each day was paid or unpaid.** Sick leave in the first 3 months of service, or beyond the entitlement, is unpaid, and the salary is prorated for those days.
- **The balance left.** The employee’s outpatient and hospitalisation balances should update when the leave is approved, so the next request is checked against the right figure.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding 14 and 60 together.** Hospitalisation leave includes outpatient leave; the yearly maximum is 60 days in total.
- **Paying sick leave in the first 3 months.** It is unpaid by law, unless your contract is more generous. If it is, write that down.
- **Ignoring pro-rating between 3 and 6 months.** An employee with 4 months of service gets 8 outpatient days, not 14.
- **Deducting sick leave from annual leave.** They are separate entitlements.
- **Not recording when the employer was told.** The 48-hour notice is an eligibility condition, so keep the date and time the request or message came in.
- **Leave balances kept on a spreadsheet.** Balances drift from payroll, and unpaid days are missed in the pay run.

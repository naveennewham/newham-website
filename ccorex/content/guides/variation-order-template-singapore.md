A variation order (VO) records a change to the work you contracted to do: something added, something taken out, or something done differently. In Singapore it usually has two parts: a form that describes the change and prices it, and a register that tracks every VO on the project from instruction to payment. Get both right and variations become easy to agree and easy to claim. Get them wrong and they become the largest source of disputes on the job.

This guide walks through the VO form, how to price a variation, and the VO register, then shows how variations flow into your progress claims. For the claim layout itself, see our [progress claim template](/resources/progress-claim-template-singapore).

## Instruction, variation order and variation claim

Three terms are often used loosely. Keeping them apart helps.

| Term | What it is | Who usually issues it |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction | The direction to change the work | Architect, superintending officer, engineer or main contractor |
| Variation order | The record of the change: scope, reason and value | The contract administrator, or you, for agreement |
| Variation claim | Your request for payment for the variation | You, usually inside a progress claim |

The standard forms used in Singapore, such as the SIA conditions, the PSSCOC for public sector work and the REDAS conditions, each set who may instruct a variation, how it must be confirmed and how it is valued. Subcontracts often pass these terms down with their own changes. Read your contract for the exact rules; the template below works with any of them.

## Part 1: The VO form

The form identifies the change and ties it to an instruction. A quantity surveyor should be able to read it without asking you a question.

| Field | Example | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Project | Project title and site | Ties the VO to the project |
| Contract / subcontract reference | Letter of award or PO number | Identifies the contract the change sits under |
| VO number | VO 012 | One running sequence per project |
| Instruction reference and date | AI 045, 3 Oct | Links the VO to the written instruction |
| Instructed by | Name and role | Shows the instruction came from someone authorised |
| Description of change | Add 14 sprinkler heads to level 3 retail units | States the scope clearly |
| Reason / cause | Revised tenant layout | Separates client changes from design development |
| Drawings and documents | Drawing no. and revision, RFI reference | Supports the scope and the quantities |
| Time impact | None / 3 working days / to be assessed | Records the effect on programme |

Write the description in the same terms as the contract BOQ where you can. If the change affects existing BOQ items, quote their references. This makes it much easier to agree the rates.

### Where the rates come from

Most contracts set an order of preference for valuing variations. A common pattern is:

1. **Contract rates.** If the varied work is the same as BOQ items, use the BOQ rates.
2. **Pro-rata or star rates.** If the work is similar but not identical, build a new rate from the BOQ rates, adjusted for the difference. Show how you built it.
3. **Fair rates or quotations.** If there is nothing comparable, price it from first principles or submit a quotation for agreement.
4. **Daywork.** If the work cannot be measured, record labour, plant and materials on daywork sheets signed on the day.

Check the order and the rules in your own contract. Some contracts allow daywork only when the contract administrator agrees in advance.

### Additions and omissions

Show omitted work as negative amounts, valued at the contract rates. Keep the additions and omissions from one instruction on the same VO so the net value is clear.

The figures are illustrative only. Whether you can add markup or preliminaries, and at what rate, depends on your contract and on how the BOQ rates were built. If your BOQ rates already include overheads and profit, adding them again will be challenged.

### GST

GST in Singapore is 9%. If you are GST-registered, variations are part of your contract value and are invoiced in the same way as contract work. Many contractors leave GST off the VO itself and add it on the progress claim. Either works, as long as you do not charge it twice. Agree the treatment with your accountant against IRAS guidance.

## Part 3: The VO register

The register is one line per VO, kept for the whole project. It answers the questions every project manager is asked: what has been instructed, what has been agreed, and what has been paid.

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| VO number | VO 012 |
| Description | Short description of the change |
| Instruction date | Date of the written instruction |
| Submitted date | Date you sent the priced VO |
| Status | Submitted, agreed, rejected or disputed |
| Submitted value | Your priced value, excluding GST |
| Agreed value | The value agreed or certified |
| Included in claim no. | The progress claim that first included it |
| Remarks | Correspondence, reasons for rejection, next step |

Review the register every month before you prepare the progress claim. Any VO still "submitted" after a few weeks needs a chase. A large total of unagreed VOs is a cash flow risk, and it should be visible to whoever signs off the claim.

## How variations flow into your claims

Once a variation is instructed, most contracts allow you to claim the work done on it in your progress claims, even before the final value is agreed. Put variations on their own sheet in the claim, with the VO number and status on every line. Our [progress claim template](/resources/progress-claim-template-singapore) has a variations sheet built for this.

Under Singapore's Security of Payment Act, a payment claim can include variation work, and the respondent must answer it in a payment response within the time the Act or the contract allows. A claim that clearly identifies each VO, its instruction and its value is easier to certify and easier to defend if it is disputed. See [SOP Act payment claims and payment responses](/resources/sop-act-payment-claims-singapore) and [progress claims in Singapore](/resources/progress-claims-singapore) for the process, and check BCA's guidance for the current rules.

## Common mistakes

1. **Working on verbal instructions.** If you act before the instruction is in writing, confirm it in writing the same day.
2. **Missing notice periods.** Many contracts require notice of a variation, or of its time and cost effect, within a set period. Late notice can cost you the claim.
3. **Mixing VOs into contract items.** Variations hidden inside contract lines are hard to certify and tend to be cut.
4. **No time impact noted.** Record the effect on programme on every VO, even if it is "none".
5. **Rates with no basis.** A new rate with no build-up invites a counter-offer. Show how you got there.
6. **Adding markup twice.** If BOQ rates already include overheads and profit, do not add them again.
7. **No register.** Without one, agreed VOs get missed from claims and rejected ones get claimed again.

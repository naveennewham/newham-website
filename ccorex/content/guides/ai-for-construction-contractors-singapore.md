For a Singapore contractor, AI is most useful today where people spend hours reading, searching and re-typing: tender documents, the client's BOQ, project records, drawings and HR admin. It works best as a first pass that a person checks, not as a decision-maker. This guide sets out the practical uses by workflow, what each one does, which are mature and which are still early, and what to check before you rely on the output.

## Where AI helps a contractor

| Workflow | What AI does | Maturity | What a person still does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading tenders | Summarises the tender documents into key fields: scope, trades, dates, conditions | In use today | Reviews and corrects the summary |
| Pricing the client's BOQ | Finds the quantity and amount columns on the client's BOQ PDF and overlays prices | In use today | Checks every figure before submission |
| Questions about your data | Answers questions across tenders, projects, purchasing and HR records | In use today | Decides what to do with the answer |
| Document search | Finds documents by meaning, not just keywords | In use today | Confirms the document is the latest revision |
| Site attendance | Verifies check-ins with face recognition and GPS | In use today | HR reviews and approves attendance |
| Drawing take-off | Proposes rooms and counts symbols on drawings | Early | Checks scale, rooms and counts |
| Invoices and receipts | Reads invoices or receipts and pre-fills the record | Early | Matches and approves |
| Cost and attendance warnings | Flags budget overruns or unusual attendance patterns | Early | Investigates and acts |

## 1. Reading and summarising tenders

Tender packages run to hundreds of pages, and the details that matter, such as scope boundaries, closing dates, liquidated damages and specified brands, are spread across them. AI can read the documents and pull these into a short summary or a set of fields, so the estimator starts from an overview instead of page one. It also helps with questions such as "what does the specification say about sprinkler pipe material?" without searching every file.

**Check:** treat the summary as a reading aid. Confirm anything that affects price or risk against the clause itself.

## 2. Pricing the client's BOQ

When the client issues its own BOQ as a PDF, the priced figures are often re-typed line by line just before closing. AI can find the quantity and amount columns on each page and place your prices on the rows to be priced, with page totals, so nothing is re-keyed.

**Check:** review every overlaid figure and the totals before the Form of Tender goes out.

## 3. Drawing take-off

AI take-off tools propose room outlines and count symbols such as light fittings, detectors and call points. On clean drawings this can save measuring time, but results depend heavily on drawing quality, scale and symbol conventions, and the technology is still maturing.

**Check:** calibrate the scale yourself, review every room and count against the drawing, and keep the estimator's corrections. See [quantity takeoff methods](/resources/quantity-takeoff-methods) for a workflow and accuracy checklist.

## 4. Asking questions of your own data

An AI assistant connected to your systems can answer questions like "which purchase requests on Block B are still pending?" or "who is on leave next week?", and turn the answer into a chart or an Excel export. This saves the time spent building reports for routine questions.

**Check:** make sure the assistant only sees your organisation's data, cannot change records, and that approvals stay with people.

## 5. Finding documents

Projects generate thousands of files: drawings, addenda, submittals, minutes and correspondence. Search by meaning finds "the addendum that changed the sprinkler spec" even when you don't know the file name.

**Check:** confirm the document found is the current revision before acting on it.

## 6. Site attendance and HR

For contractors with site teams, AI face recognition on each worker's phone, combined with GPS, verifies who checked in and where, without a kiosk at every site gate. Attendance then flows into payroll after HR approval. Newer uses, such as reading receipts for expense claims and flagging unusual attendance patterns, are still early.

**Check:** HR should review and approve attendance before payroll, and payroll should still apply the [MOM rules for overtime](/resources/overtime-calculation-singapore) and [CPF](/resources/how-to-calculate-cpf-contributions) correctly.

## Questions to ask before you buy AI software

- What data can the AI see, and is it limited to your organisation?
- Can it change or approve records, or only read them?
- Where are prompts and answers stored, and are they encrypted?
- How is usage controlled, so costs stay predictable?
- Which features are in production today, and which are early access?
- Can you test it on your own tender documents and drawings before you commit?

## Common mistakes

- Submitting AI-prepared figures without a person checking them
- Trusting a summary over the actual clause on price-sensitive points
- Running AI take-off without calibrating the scale
- Buying for a feature that is still in early access
- Giving an AI tool access to data it doesn't need
- Measuring nothing before and after, so nobody knows whether it saved time

Under MOM's rules, employers must **pay salary at least once a month**, **within 7 days after the end of the salary period**, and **overtime within 14 days**. Only deductions the Employment Act allows may be made, and **total deductions in a salary period cannot exceed 50% of the salary payable**, with a few exceptions. Final pay has its own deadlines, depending on how employment ends.

These rules apply to employees covered by the Employment Act. Figures are as of October 2026, from MOM's salary pages.

## Payment deadlines

| What | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Salary | At least once a month, within 7 days after the end of the salary period |
| Overtime pay | Within 14 days after the end of the salary period |
| How | Into the employee's bank account, or by cheque (paid once it clears) |
| When | On a working day, during working hours |

**Example.** For a salary period of 1 to 31 January, salary is due by 7 February and overtime for January by 14 February.

## Final salary when employment ends

| Situation | Final salary due |
|---|---|
| Employee resigns and serves notice | On the last day of employment |
| Employee resigns without serving notice | Within 7 days of the last day |
| Employer ends the contract | On the last day, or within 3 working days if that is not possible |
| Dismissal for misconduct | On the last day, or within 3 working days of dismissal if that is not possible |
| Foreign employee leaving | Money due, including salary and leave pay, withheld for up to 30 days for tax clearance |

## Allowed deductions

As of October 2026, MOM says salary may be deducted only for:

- Court orders, or where the employer is appointed to recover income tax, property tax or GST.
- Absence from work.
- Damage to or loss of money or goods the employee is responsible for.
- Accommodation the employee has accepted.
- Amenities and services the employee has accepted and the Commissioner for Labour has authorised.
- Recovery of advances, loans, overpaid salary or unearned benefits.
- The employee's share of CPF, from the right month's salary.
- Payments to a registered co-operative society, with written consent.
- Other purposes the employee consents to in writing, if they benefit the employee. The employee can withdraw that consent at any time.

## Deduction limits

| Deduction | Limit |
|---|---|
| All deductions in one salary period | 50% of the salary payable |
| Not counted in the 50% | Absence; recovery of advances, loans, overpaid salary or unearned benefits; consented co-operative society payments |
| Damage or loss | 25% of one month's salary, as a one-time lump sum, after an inquiry |
| Accommodation, amenities and services | The value supplied or 25% of the salary for the period, with the Commissioner for Labour's approval |
| Advance repayments | Instalments over no more than 12 months, each up to 25% of the period's salary |
| Loan repayments | Instalments, each up to 25% of the period's salary |

On the final salary payment, total authorised deductions may exceed 50%.

For migrant workers, MOM says the employer needs the worker's written consent and must notify MOM before reducing salary or making new or larger deductions.

## Common mistakes

- **Paying overtime with the next month's salary.** Overtime is due within 14 days of the end of the salary period in which it was worked.
- **Deducting for lateness or damage without the rules.** Damage deductions need an inquiry and are capped at 25% of a month's salary.
- **Going over 50%.** Add up every deduction for the period, apart from the exempt ones, before you finalise the pay run.
- **Deducting for liquidated damages.** It is not allowed, because it does not benefit the employee.
- **Not itemising deductions on the payslip.** Every deduction should appear on the itemised payslip.

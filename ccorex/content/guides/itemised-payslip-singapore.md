Employers in Singapore must give every employee covered by the Employment Act an **itemised payslip**. It has to show up to **12 items**, such as basic salary, allowances, deductions and overtime, and go out **with the salary payment** or **within three working days** of it. It can be a soft or hard copy, even handwritten. Payslip records must be kept for **two years**.

This guide lists what MOM requires, then shows a sample payslip, the record-keeping rules and the mistakes that cause disputes. As of October 2026, the requirements are those on MOM’s [itemised payslips](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/salary/itemised-payslips) page. Check it before you rely on them.

## Who must get an itemised payslip

All employees **covered by the Employment Act** must get one. For employees outside the Act, a payslip is still good practice and makes salary queries easier to answer.

## When to issue payslips

| Situation | When the payslip is due |
|---|---|
| Normal pay run | Together with the salary payment |
| If issuing with payment isn’t possible | Within three working days of payment |
| Employment ends (termination or dismissal) | Together with the outstanding salary |

A payslip can cover more than one payment, for example when overtime is paid separately from basic salary in the same month, as long as each payment date is shown.

## The 12 items MOM requires

As of October 2026, MOM says an itemised payslip must include these items, **unless an item doesn’t apply** to the employee:

| # | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full name of employer | |
| 2 | Full name of employee | |
| 3 | Date of payment | Or dates, if the payslip combines several payments |
| 4 | Basic salary | For hourly, daily or piece-rated employees, also the basic rate and the hours, days or pieces |
| 5 | Start and end dates of the salary period | |
| 6 | Allowances paid for the period | Fixed and ad hoc, such as transport |
| 7 | Any other additional payments | Such as bonuses, rest day pay and public holiday pay |
| 8 | Deductions for the period | Fixed and ad hoc, such as the employee’s CPF and unpaid leave |
| 9 | Overtime hours worked | |
| 10 | Overtime pay | |
| 11 | Start and end dates of the overtime period | Only if different from the salary period |
| 12 | Net salary paid | |

An employee who isn’t paid overtime, such as a manager outside Part 4 of the Employment Act, doesn’t need items 9 to 11.

## Sample itemised payslip

The figures below are an **illustration** for a Singapore citizen workman aged 30 on a monthly basic salary of $3,000, with a $200 transport allowance and 10 overtime hours. They use the CPF rates and overtime formula in our [CPF](/resources/how-to-calculate-cpf-contributions) and [overtime](/resources/overtime-calculation-singapore) guides. They are not a quote of anyone’s pay.

| Item | Detail | Amount (SGD) |
|---|---|---|
| Employer | Your company’s full registered name | |
| Employee | Employee’s full name | |
| Salary period | 1–31 March 2027 | |
| Date of payment | 7 April 2027 | |
| Basic salary | Monthly | 3,000.00 |
| Allowances | Transport | 200.00 |
| Overtime | 10 hours at $23.60 | 236.00 |
| Overtime period | Same as salary period | |
| **Gross pay** | | **3,436.00** |
| Deductions | Employee CPF (20% of $3,436, cents dropped) | (687.00) |
| **Net salary paid** | | **2,749.00** |

How the overtime rate is worked out: the hourly basic rate is (12 × $3,000) ÷ (52 × 44) = $15.73, and 1.5 × $15.73 = $23.60. Allowances are left out of the overtime rate.

Employer CPF, SDL and any self-help group fund deduction are worked out on the same pay run. Employer CPF and SDL are not deducted from the employee’s pay, but many employers show them on the payslip so the employee can see the total.

## Keeping payslip records

As of October 2026, MOM says employers must keep payslip records:

- For **current employees**: the latest **two years**.
- For **ex-employees**: the last **two years**, kept for **one year after** the employee leaves.

Keep them so you can find a single employee’s payslips quickly. Salary claims and MOM queries usually ask for specific months.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving allowances and deductions as one total.** MOM asks for allowances and deductions for the period; show each line so the employee can check it.
- **No salary period or payment date.** These are required items and the first thing checked in a dispute.
- **Overtime missing or lumped into basic pay.** Show overtime hours and overtime pay separately.
- **Payslips issued weeks after payday.** The deadline is with the payment, or within three working days of it.
- **No payslip on the last day.** When employment ends, the payslip goes with the outstanding salary.
- **Records lost when an employee leaves.** Keep ex-employees’ records for a year after they go.
- **Editing old payslips after a back-dated salary change.** Pay the difference as an arrears line in the next run, and leave the original payslip as it was issued.

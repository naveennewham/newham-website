Leave entitlement in Singapore is set by the Employment Act and, for parental leave, by the Child Development Co-Savings Act. Most statutory leave starts after **3 months of service**, and the amounts grow with length of service. This guide sets out the main entitlements as the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) publishes them, and what HR should track so leave and payroll agree.

The figures below are summarised from MOM's pages in October 2026. Rules change, so check the linked MOM page before you rely on a number.

## Annual leave

Employees covered by the Employment Act who have worked for at least 3 months are entitled to paid annual leave. Year of service starts on the day the employee starts work. Source: [MOM, annual leave eligibility and entitlement](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave/annual-leave/eligibility-and-entitlement).

| Year of service | Days of annual leave |
|---|---|
| 1st | 7 |
| 2nd | 8 |
| 3rd | 9 |
| 4th | 10 |
| 5th | 11 |
| 6th | 12 |
| 7th | 13 |
| 8th and after | 14 |

For an employee who has worked between 3 and 12 months, leave is based on the number of completed months worked. MOM notes this applies even while the employee is on probation.

## Sick leave and hospitalisation leave

Paid sick leave depends on completed months of service. The employee must be covered by the Employment Act, have served at least 3 months, be certified unfit for work by a registered medical practitioner, and inform (or try to inform) the employer within 48 hours. Source: [MOM, sick leave eligibility and entitlement](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave/sick-leave/eligibility-and-entitlement).

| Months of service | Outpatient sick leave (days) | Hospitalisation leave (days) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5 | 15 |
| 4 | 8 | 30 |
| 5 | 11 | 45 |
| 6 or more | 14 | 60 |

The 60 days of hospitalisation leave include the 14 days of outpatient sick leave, so they are not added together.

## Childcare leave

Source: [MOM, childcare leave eligibility and entitlement](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave/childcare-leave/eligibility-and-entitlement).

| Child | Entitlement | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore citizen, below 7 | 6 days a year for each parent | 42 days per child |
| Non-citizen, below 7 | 2 days a year (Employment Act) | 14 days per child |

The employee must have served the employer for at least 3 continuous months. Parents whose youngest Singapore citizen child is aged 7 to 12 may be eligible for 2 days of extended childcare leave. MOM notes that an increase in childcare leave was announced at the 2026 National Day Rally, with the implementation date and criteria to be released later, so check the MOM page for the current position.

## Maternity leave

Source: [MOM, maternity leave eligibility and entitlement](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave/maternity-leave/eligibility-and-entitlement).

- **16 weeks** of Government-Paid Maternity Leave when the child is a Singapore citizen and the mother has served the employer for at least 3 continuous months before the birth. She should give at least 4 weeks’ notice, or tell the employer as soon as possible after delivery.
- **12 weeks** under the Employment Act otherwise, for employees covered by the Act with at least 3 months’ continuous service before the birth. The employer pays the first 8 weeks if the employee has fewer than 2 living children and gave at least 1 week’s notice; the last 4 weeks are unpaid unless the contract says otherwise.

Paternity, shared parental, adoption and unpaid infant care leave have their own rules. See MOM’s [leave index](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/leave) for each one.

## Public holidays

Singapore’s public holidays are gazetted each year: 2026 has 10 public holidays over 11 days. When a public holiday falls on a Sunday, MOM lists the following Monday as a public holiday for employees whose rest day falls on that Sunday (for example, Monday 9 November 2026 for Deepavali on Sunday 8 November). Source: [MOM, public holidays](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays).

## What HR should track

A leave system only helps if it holds the facts that decide each entitlement and passes the result to payroll:

1. **Start date and service months** for each employee, because annual, sick and childcare leave all depend on them.
2. **Leave types and entitlements** that match your contracts, which may be more generous than the Employment Act.
3. **Approvals with a trail**, so you can show who approved each day of leave.
4. **Public holidays** for the year, excluded from leave days.
5. **Unpaid leave in payroll**, so the salary is prorated for the days not paid.

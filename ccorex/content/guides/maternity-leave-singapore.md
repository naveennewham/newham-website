Maternity leave in Singapore comes in two forms. If the child is a **Singapore citizen**, an eligible mother gets **16 weeks of Government-Paid Maternity Leave (GPML)**. If the child is not a Singapore citizen, a mother covered by the **Employment Act** can get **12 weeks**. In both cases she must have served the employer for at least 3 continuous months before the birth. The employer pays the salary during the leave and claims back the Government-paid weeks.

This guide covers who qualifies, how many weeks are paid by whom, the notice rules and what HR should record. Figures are as of October 2026; MOM has announced changes, so check its maternity leave pages before you update your policy.

## GPML or Employment Act leave?

| | Government-Paid Maternity Leave | Employment Act maternity leave |
|---|---|---|
| Child's citizenship | Singapore citizen | Not a Singapore citizen |
| Length | 16 weeks | 12 weeks |
| Service before the birth | At least 3 continuous months | At least 3 continuous months |
| Other conditions | — | Covered by the Employment Act; for the paid 8 weeks, fewer than 2 living children at delivery |
| Notice | At least 4 weeks | At least 1 week |
| Law | Child Development Co-Savings Act | Part 9 of the Employment Act |

A mother of a non-citizen child who is not covered by the Employment Act gets maternity leave on the terms of her contract.

## Who pays which weeks

As of October 2026, MOM says:

| Situation | Paid by the employer | Reimbursed by the Government |
|---|---|---|
| GPML, first or second child | First 8 weeks, at the gross rate of pay | Last 8 weeks, capped at $10,000 per 4 weeks ($20,000 per child) |
| GPML, third or later child | Paid out first, then claimed back | All 16 weeks, capped at $10,000 per 4 weeks ($40,000 per child) |
| Employment Act, 12 weeks | First 8 weeks at the usual salary, if eligible | — |

Under the Employment Act, the last 4 weeks are unpaid unless the contract provides for them.

The employer keeps paying the employee as normal and claims the Government-paid portion through the Government-Paid Leave portal run by MSF.

## Notice and what happens without it

- **GPML:** the employee should give at least **4 weeks' notice**. If she cannot, she should tell the employer as soon as possible after the delivery.
- **Employment Act:** at least **1 week's notice**, and inform the employer as soon as possible after the delivery.

MOM says that without the required notice and without a good reason, the employee may get only **half pay** during the leave.

## Changes announced for Singapore citizen children

At the 2026 National Day Rally, the Government announced it will reimburse employers for the **full duration** of maternity leave for parents of Singapore citizen children, up to the reimbursement limit. As of October 2026, MOM had not released the start date or the criteria. Until it does, apply the current rules above.

## What HR should record

- The child's expected date of delivery and actual date of birth.
- The child's citizenship, which decides between GPML and Employment Act leave.
- The employee's start date, to confirm 3 continuous months of service.
- The date notice was given.
- The weeks taken and the pay for each period, so the Government-paid weeks can be claimed.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying the Employment Act 12 weeks to a citizen child.** If the child is a Singapore citizen and the mother is eligible, GPML gives 16 weeks.
- **Stopping salary during GPML.** The employer pays the employee and then claims the Government-paid weeks; the employee should not wait for the reimbursement.
- **Forgetting the cap.** Reimbursement is capped at $10,000 per 4 weeks, so a high earner's salary above the cap is the employer's cost.
- **Not keeping the notice date.** The notice rules affect pay, so record when and how the employee told you.
- **Changing your policy before MOM publishes the details.** The announced full reimbursement has no start date yet.

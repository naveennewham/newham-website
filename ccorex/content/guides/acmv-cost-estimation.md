ACMV cost estimation means breaking the air-conditioning and mechanical ventilation scope into equipment, distribution (ductwork and pipework), terminals, insulation, controls and commissioning, measuring each from the drawings and schedules, and pricing material and installation separately. The equipment schedule usually drives the biggest single costs, while ductwork, pipework and insulation drive most of the measuring effort. This guide sets out a structure, step-by-step method and checks for ACMV estimation on tender.

## What is in an ACMV scope

ACMV packages vary by building type, but most tenders include some combination of:

- **Central plant**: chillers, cooling towers, pumps, heat exchangers
- **Direct expansion (DX) systems**: VRF or VRV systems, split units, condensing units
- **Air handling**: AHUs, PAHUs, FCUs
- **Mechanical ventilation**: supply, exhaust and transfer fans; toilet and kitchen exhaust; car park ventilation
- **Smoke control**: smoke extract and pressurisation fans and associated ductwork and dampers, where required
- **Distribution**: ductwork, chilled water pipework, refrigerant piping, condensate drains
- **Terminals**: diffusers, grilles, registers, louvres
- **Dampers and accessories**: volume control dampers, fire dampers, motorised dampers, flexible connections, sound attenuators
- **Insulation**: duct and pipe insulation, cladding where exposed
- **Controls**: thermostats, sensors, control panels, interface with the building management system
- **Testing, commissioning and balancing**

Before measuring, confirm what is in your scope and what is by others. Power supply to equipment, builder's works (openings, plinths, access panels) and BMS integration are frequent sources of scope gaps.

## Codes and authority requirements

In Singapore, ACMV design is governed by SS 553, the code of practice for air-conditioning and mechanical ventilation in buildings, together with energy efficiency requirements under BCA's regulations. Smoke control systems and fire dampers also fall under SCDF fire safety requirements. As an estimator you are not usually checking the design for compliance, but you do need to price what compliance requires: fire-rated items where specified, testing and commissioning, and authority submissions if they are in your scope. Read the specification to see which of these sit with you.

## Step-by-step ACMV estimation

### 1. Review the tender documents

Read the ACMV specification, equipment schedules, drawings and any performance requirements. Note specified brands, "or equivalent" clauses, efficiency requirements and warranty periods. On Design & Build tenders, note the design parameters you are expected to meet.

### 2. Set up the estimate structure

Break the estimate down by building and floor, then by system:

1. Central plant or outdoor units
2. Air-side equipment per floor
3. Ductwork and accessories per floor
4. Pipework per floor and risers
5. Terminals per area
6. Insulation
7. Controls
8. Testing, commissioning and balancing
9. Preliminaries

### 3. Price equipment from the schedule

Take each item from the equipment schedule with its capacity, quantity and location. Major equipment is usually priced from current supplier quotes against the specification. Check that quotes cover the full specification: accessories, controls cards, vibration isolators, delivery and lifting.

### 4. Measure ductwork

Measure duct runs by size from the layout drawings. Depending on the method of measurement, convert to surface area or weight, and decide whether fittings (bends, tees, reducers, transitions) are measured separately or included through an allowance. Add accessories: volume control dampers, fire dampers, access doors, flexible connections and supports.

### 5. Measure pipework

Measure chilled water, condenser water, refrigerant and condensate piping by size and material. Include risers and connections to equipment. Count valves, strainers, gauges and other accessories at each equipment connection from schematics.

### 6. Count terminals

Count diffusers, grilles and registers by type and size, area by area. Cross-check against the room schedule so no room is missed.

### 7. Measure insulation

Measure duct insulation (typically by area) and pipe insulation (typically by length per pipe size), following the specification for thickness, material and finishes. Add cladding where insulation is exposed outdoors or in plant rooms.

### 8. Add controls and commissioning

Price thermostats, sensors, control panels and wiring in your scope. Add testing, commissioning, air and water balancing, and documentation such as as-built drawings and operation and maintenance manuals.

### 9. Apply markups and build the summary

Apply material and installation markups, add preliminaries and produce the summary with GST shown separately at the prevailing rate.

## ACMV estimate structure

| Section | Typical unit | Pricing source |
|---|---|---|
| Chillers, AHUs, VRF systems | nr / set | Supplier quotes |
| Fans | nr | Supplier quotes or library |
| Ductwork | m² or kg | Rate library |
| Duct accessories, dampers | nr | Rate library |
| Pipework | m (by size) | Rate library |
| Valves and fittings | nr | Rate library |
| Diffusers, grilles | nr | Rate library |
| Insulation | m / m² | Rate library or subcontract quote |
| Controls | lot / nr | Supplier or specialist quote |
| Testing and commissioning | item | Internal estimate |

Splitting each rate into material and installation helps here. Equipment is mostly material cost; ductwork installation can be a large share of the line. Separate markups let you reflect that.

## ACMV estimation checklist

- [ ] Equipment schedule reconciled with drawings (quantity and location)
- [ ] Supplier quotes cover the specified capacity and accessories
- [ ] Ductwork measured by size, with method for fittings stated
- [ ] Fire dampers counted at every fire-rated wall or floor penetration shown
- [ ] Pipework risers and equipment connections included
- [ ] Condensate drains included for every indoor unit
- [ ] Insulation specification (thickness, material, finish) applied correctly
- [ ] Every room in the schedule has terminals or a note why not
- [ ] Lifting, hoisting and delivery to plant rooms or roof considered
- [ ] Controls scope and BMS interface boundaries clear
- [ ] Testing, commissioning and balancing included
- [ ] Exclusions listed (power supply, builder's works, as applicable)

## Questions to raise during the tender

If the documents leave any of these open, send a tender query or state your assumption in the qualifications:

- Is power supply to outdoor units, AHUs and fans by the electrical contractor or by you?
- Who provides openings, plinths, access panels and supports from the structure?
- Are fire dampers and smoke control ductwork in the ACMV package or a separate one?
- What is the scope of BMS integration: points list, protocol, who supplies the gateway?
- Are refrigerant piping lengths for VRF or split systems shown, or left for the contractor to design?
- Which testing, commissioning and balancing standards and witnesses are required?

Clear answers reduce the risk of pricing the same item twice or not at all.

## Common mistakes

1. **Pricing equipment from an outdated quote.** Equipment prices and lead times change. Get current quotes against the specified model and capacity.
2. **Missing accessories on equipment.** Isolators, flexible connections, drain pans and controls cards are easy to leave out of a quote.
3. **Ignoring duct fittings.** If fittings are not measured and not covered by an allowance, the ductwork cost is understated.
4. **Missing risers and vertical runs.** Plans show horizontal runs only. Use schematics and sections for risers.
5. **Insulation on the wrong basis.** Pricing all pipe insulation at one thickness when the specification varies by pipe size or service.
6. **Unclear scope boundaries.** Assuming power supply, BMS integration or builder's works are by others without stating so in the tender.
7. **Leaving out commissioning and balancing.** These take time on site and need to be priced.

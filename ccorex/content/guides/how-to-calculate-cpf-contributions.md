To calculate CPF for an employee in Singapore, take their wages for the month (ordinary wages up to the **$8,000 Ordinary Wage ceiling**, plus any additional wages within the annual limit), apply the **contribution rate for their age band**, round the total to the nearest dollar, and split it into the employee’s share (cents dropped) and the employer’s share (the rest). For an employee aged 55 or below earning more than $750 a month, the total in 2026 is **37%: 17% employer and 20% employee**.

This guide walks through each step with the CPF Board’s figures, then covers the Skills Development Levy (SDL) and self-help group funds that sit on the same payslip. Figures are as published by the CPF Board in October 2026; check the linked pages before you rely on them.

## Step 1: Check whether CPF applies

Employers must pay CPF for employees who are **Singapore Citizens or Singapore Permanent Residents** and earn **more than $50** in total wages a month. Source: [CPF Board, how much CPF contributions to pay](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/how-much-cpf-contributions-to-pay).

- Above $50 but not more than $500 a month: only the employer share is payable.
- Above $500 and up to $750: the employee share is phased in. Use the CPF Board’s contribution calculator for these wages.
- First- and second-year PRs: graduated (lower) rates apply unless the employer and employee jointly apply for full rates, or the employer pays full rates while the employee pays graduated rates.
- Foreign employees on work passes do not contribute to CPF.

## Step 2: Work out the wages that attract CPF

CPF separates **Ordinary Wages** (regular monthly pay, such as salary and fixed allowances) from **Additional Wages** (such as bonuses).

| Ceiling | Amount from 1 January 2026 |
|---|---|
| Ordinary Wage ceiling | $8,000 a month |
| Annual salary ceiling (Ordinary and Additional Wages) | $102,000 a year |

Ordinary wages above $8,000 in a month do not attract CPF. The Additional Wage ceiling is the $102,000 annual salary ceiling less the total ordinary wages subject to CPF in the year. Source: [CPF Board, what is the Ordinary Wage ceiling](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/what-is-the-ordinary-wage-ow-ceiling).

The ceiling rose in steps: $6,000 until August 2023, $6,300 from September 2023, $6,800 in 2024, $7,400 in 2025 and $8,000 from 2026. If you are checking an older payslip, use the ceiling for that month.

## Step 3: Apply the rate for the employee’s age

For Singapore Citizens and PRs from their third year, earning more than $750 a month, from 1 January 2026:

| Employee’s age | Employer | Employee | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 55 and below | 17% | 20% | 37% |
| Above 55 to 60 | 16% | 18% | 34% |
| Above 60 to 65 | 12.5% | 12.5% | 25% |
| Above 65 to 70 | 9% | 7.5% | 16.5% |
| Above 70 | 7.5% | 5% | 12.5% |

Source: [CPF Board, how much CPF contributions to pay](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/how-much-cpf-contributions-to-pay). The CPF Board has also announced changes to the rates for employees above 55 from 1 January 2027; see its [contribution rate changes page](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/conrate2023).

## Step 4: Round and split the contribution

The CPF Board’s rounding rules:

1. Round the **total** contribution to the nearest dollar: drop amounts under 50 cents, and treat 50 cents and above as an extra dollar.
2. Calculate the **employee’s share** and drop the cents.
3. The **employer’s share** is the total minus the employee’s share.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Singapore Citizen, age 30, monthly salary $6,000, no bonus.**

- Total: 37% × $6,000 = $2,220
- Employee share: 20% × $6,000 = $1,200 (deducted from pay)
- Employer share: $2,220 − $1,200 = $1,020
- Take-home before other deductions: $6,000 − $1,200 = $4,800

**Example 2: Singapore Citizen, age 40, monthly salary $10,000.**

- Ordinary wages subject to CPF are capped at $8,000.
- Total: 37% × $8,000 = $2,960
- Employee share: 20% × $8,000 = $1,600
- Employer share: $2,960 − $1,600 = $1,360

For bonuses, part-year employment and older employees, use the CPF Board’s calculators under the employer section of cpf.gov.sg, including the [Additional Wage ceiling calculator](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/tools-and-services/calculators/additional-wage-ceiling-calculator).

## The other contributions on a Singapore payslip

**Skills Development Levy (SDL).** Paid by the employer for each employee, at 0.25% of monthly remuneration, with a minimum of $2 (for employees earning less than $800) and a maximum of $11.25 (for those earning more than $4,500). It is collected by the CPF Board for SkillsFuture Singapore and is not deducted from the employee’s pay. Source: [GoBusiness, about SDL](https://www.gobusiness.gov.sg/skills-development-levy-faqs/sdl-about).

**Self-help group funds.** Monthly contributions to CDAC, SINDA, MBMF or ECF are deducted from the employee’s wages according to their race or religion, and paid together with CPF. Employees can opt out of some funds; the amounts depend on monthly wages.

**Foreign worker levy.** For Work Permit and S Pass holders the employer pays a monthly levy to MOM instead of CPF. Rates depend on the sector, the pass and the worker’s skill level; see MOM’s levy pages.

## Avoiding CPF mistakes

The most common errors come from inputs, not arithmetic: the wrong age band after a birthday, a PR’s graduated rate applied after the second year, a bonus pushed over the annual ceiling, or unpaid leave not deducted before CPF is worked out. Payroll software helps because it holds the employee’s date of birth, residency status and approved attendance, and applies the current rates on every run.

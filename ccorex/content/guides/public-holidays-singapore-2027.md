Singapore has **11 public holidays in 2027**: New Year’s Day, two days of Chinese New Year, Hari Raya Puasa, Good Friday, Labour Day, Hari Raya Haji, Vesak Day, National Day, Deepavali and Christmas Day. One falls on a Sunday (the second day of Chinese New Year, 7 February), so **Monday 8 February 2027** is a public holiday for employees whose rest day is that Sunday. Three fall on a Saturday.

This guide lists the 2027 dates, then sets out the Employment Act rules employers need for payroll: who gets paid public holidays, what happens when a holiday lands on a rest day or non-working day, and how to pay employees who work on one. The dates are as published on MOM’s [public holidays](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays) page in October 2026, and the rules come from MOM’s [public holidays entitlement and pay](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays-entitlement-and-pay) page. Check both before you plan the year.

### The rest of 2026

For the remainder of this year, MOM lists **Deepavali on Sunday 8 November 2026**, with **Monday 9 November** a public holiday for employees whose rest day falls on 8 November, and **Christmas Day on Friday 25 December 2026**.

## Who gets paid public holidays

As of October 2026, MOM says employees covered by the **Employment Act** are entitled to **11 paid public holidays a year**, paid at the gross rate of pay. Employees not covered by the Act follow their contract.

There is **no public holiday pay** if the employee:

- Was absent without the employer’s consent or a reasonable excuse on the working day **just before or just after** the holiday, or
- Was on approved **unpaid leave** on the holiday.

Authorised leave on the day before or after, such as sick leave or annual leave, does not affect holiday pay.

## When a holiday falls on a rest day or non-working day

| The holiday falls on | What the employee gets |
|---|---|
| A working day | A paid day off |
| A rest day (often Sunday) | The next working day is a paid holiday |
| A non-working day (such as Saturday on a 5-day week) | Another day off, or one extra day’s salary at the gross rate |

The **rest day** and the **non-working day** are different. The rest day is the one unpaid day off a week the Employment Act requires. A non-working day is a day the employee does not normally work, such as Saturday for someone on a Monday-to-Friday week. Set both in each employee’s work schedule so payroll applies the right rule.

## Working on a public holiday

As of October 2026, MOM says an employee covered by the Employment Act who works on a public holiday gets:

- **An extra day’s salary at the basic rate.** The monthly salary already covers the holiday itself, so the employer pays one additional day.
- **Overtime** for any hours beyond the normal daily hours, at 1.5 times the hourly basic rate for employees covered by Part 4 of the Act. These hours count toward the 72-hour monthly overtime limit.

By mutual agreement, the employer and employee can **substitute another working day** for the public holiday instead. For employees **not** covered by Part 4, time off in lieu is also an option, and MOM gives a default where no duration is agreed: 4 hours off for 4 hours of work or less, or a full day off for longer.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the Monday after a Sunday holiday.** In 2027 that is Monday 8 February.
- **Ignoring Saturday holidays.** Three fall on a Saturday in 2027. Employees on a 5-day week are owed another day off or an extra day’s gross pay for each.
- **Paying holiday work at the gross rate instead of the basic rate.** The extra day for working on a public holiday is at the basic rate; the extra day for a holiday on a non-working day is at the gross rate.
- **Missing overtime on public holiday shifts.** Hours past the normal day are overtime and count toward the 72-hour cap.
- **Paying holiday pay during unpaid leave.** There is no holiday pay when the holiday falls within approved unpaid leave.
- **Counting public holidays as leave days.** A public holiday inside a block of annual leave should not be taken off the leave balance.

Fire protection estimation starts with the area schedule and hazard classification, because they decide what sprinkler heads, spacing and pipe sizes you are pricing. From there you take off heads, pipework, valves, fire alarm devices, hose reels, risers and equipment area by area, price each item for material and installation, and add testing, commissioning and authority-related work. This guide sets out the steps, a BOQ structure and the checks that catch the most common errors.

## What is in a fire protection scope

A fire protection package can include some or all of:

- **Automatic sprinkler system**: sprinkler heads, branch and range pipes, mains, risers, control valve sets, flow switches, test and drain points, alarm valves
- **Fire alarm and detection**: fire alarm panel, smoke and heat detectors, manual call points, alarm bells or sounders, interface modules, cabling
- **Hose reel system**: hose reels, pipework, valves
- **Dry or wet riser system**: risers, landing valves, breeching inlets
- **Pumps and tanks**: sprinkler pumps, hose reel pumps, jockey pumps, controllers, water storage (where in scope)
- **Gas suppression**: for server rooms, switch rooms and similar, where specified
- **Portable equipment**: fire extinguishers and signage
- **Testing and commissioning**, and attendance at inspections

Check the scope split carefully. Power supply to pumps and panels, tank construction, builder's works and interfaces with other systems (lift homing, ACMV shutdown, door release) are often split between trades.

## Codes and the Singapore context

In Singapore, fire safety requirements for buildings are set by SCDF through the Fire Code. Sprinkler systems are designed to SS CP 52, the code of practice for automatic fire sprinkler systems, and fire alarm systems to SS 645, the code of practice for the installation and servicing of electrical fire alarm systems. Smoke control and ventilation interfaces also link to SS 553 for ACMV.

You are rarely checking the design itself at tender, but you need to know enough to spot when the drawings and the stated hazard class do not match, or when a required item is missing. Always use the code editions named in the specification.

## Hazard classification and why it drives cost

Sprinkler design standards group occupancies into hazard classes, broadly light, ordinary (in several groups) and high hazard. The hazard class of an area affects:

- The type and temperature rating of sprinkler head
- The maximum area each head can cover, and so the number of heads
- Design density and the hydraulic demand, and so pipe sizes, pump duty and water storage

For estimating, this means every area in your schedule should carry a hazard class. If a car park, store room or plant room is priced at the same hazard class as an office, the quantities and pipe sizes will be wrong.

## Step-by-step fire protection estimation

### 1. Read the specification and drawings

Note the systems in scope, approved brands, head types and finishes, pipe materials and jointing methods, testing requirements and any authority-related obligations.

### 2. Build the area schedule

List each building, floor and room or zone, with its hazard class and ceiling type. Concealed, exposed and sidewall heads are priced differently, and false ceilings may require heads above and below.

### 3. Take off sprinkler heads

Count heads by type, finish and temperature rating per area. Check counts against room areas: a large room with very few heads is worth a second look.

### 4. Measure pipework

Measure pipework by size and material: range pipes, branches, mains and risers. Include drops to heads. State whether fittings are measured or included in the rate.

### 5. Count valves and accessories

From the schematic, count control valve sets, alarm valves, flow and tamper switches, test and drain assemblies, pressure gauges and isolation valves per zone.

### 6. Take off fire alarm devices

Count detectors by type, manual call points, alarm sounders, interface and monitor modules per area. Measure cabling by type from the panel to devices, including risers.

### 7. Add hose reels, risers and extinguishers

Count hose reels and landing valves per floor, measure riser pipework and count breeching inlets and extinguishers.

### 8. Price pumps, panels and equipment

Price pump sets, controllers and fire alarm panels from supplier quotes against the specification. Check that quotes include accessories and testing.

### 9. Add testing, commissioning and preliminaries

Include hydrostatic tests, flushing, flow tests, fire alarm cause-and-effect testing, attendance at inspections, as-built drawings and O&M manuals, as required by the specification.

### 10. Build the summary

Apply material and installation markups, preliminaries and show GST separately at the prevailing rate.

## Fire protection BOQ structure

| Section | Typical items | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinklers | Heads by type, drops | nr |
| Sprinkler pipework | Pipes by size, fittings | m / nr |
| Valves and accessories | Control valve sets, flow switches | nr / set |
| Fire alarm | Panel, detectors, call points, sounders | nr |
| Fire alarm cabling | Cable by type | m |
| Hose reels and risers | Hose reels, landing valves, riser pipe | nr / m |
| Pumps and tanks | Pump sets, controllers | set |
| Extinguishers and signage | By type | nr |
| Testing and commissioning | Tests, attendance, documents | item |

Group items by building and floor within each section so quantities can be checked against drawings and used later for progress claims.

## Fire protection estimating checklist

- [ ] Every area has a hazard class recorded
- [ ] Sprinkler head type matches the hazard class and ceiling type
- [ ] Heads above false ceilings and in concealed spaces considered where shown
- [ ] Pipe sizes and materials match the specification
- [ ] Drops and risers included
- [ ] Control valve sets and flow switches per zone counted
- [ ] Fire alarm devices counted per area, cabling measured to the panel
- [ ] Interfaces with other systems (lifts, ACMV, doors) priced or excluded
- [ ] Pumps and panels priced from current quotes
- [ ] Testing, commissioning and inspection attendance included
- [ ] Exclusions and assumptions listed

## Questions to raise during the tender

Where the documents are silent or unclear, send a tender query or state your assumption:

- Is the hazard class for each area confirmed, or is the contractor expected to classify it?
- Are fire pumps, controllers and water tanks in the fire protection package, and who builds the tank?
- Who provides power supply to pumps and the fire alarm panel?
- Which interfaces does the fire alarm system have to drive (lifts, ACMV, access control doors, gas suppression), and who supplies the modules?
- Are plan submissions and attendance at authority inspections in your scope?
- Is hydraulic calculation required at tender, or only after award?

Each answer can change quantities, materials or the attendance you need to price.

## Common mistakes

1. **One hazard class for the whole building.** Mixed-use buildings often contain several hazard classes. Price each area on its own class.
2. **Wrong head type.** Pricing standard heads where concealed or special-application heads are specified, or the reverse.
3. **Missing drops and risers.** Plan views do not show vertical pipework.
4. **Forgetting interface items.** Monitor and control modules for lifts, ACMV shutdown and door release add up.
5. **Underpricing testing and inspections.** Testing and attendance take labour time that is easy to leave out.
6. **Ignoring revisions.** A change of room use can change hazard class, which ripples through heads, pipe sizes and pump duty.

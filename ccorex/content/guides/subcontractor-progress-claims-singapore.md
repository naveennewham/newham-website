As a main contractor you sit on both sides of every claim. You claim from your client, and your subcontractors claim from you. This guide covers the second side: how to assess a subcontractor's progress claim fairly and quickly, what you can deduct, and how to answer it so that your position holds if the claim is disputed.

The legal framework is Singapore's Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act (the SOP Act). We cover its deadlines and the adjudication process in detail in [SOP Act payment claims and payment responses](/resources/sop-act-payment-claims-singapore). Here we focus on the assessment itself.

## Two rules to start with

**Pay-when-paid does not work.** Section 9 of the SOP Act makes pay-when-paid provisions unenforceable. You cannot hold back a subcontractor's payment because your client has not paid you. Plan your cash on that basis.

**Speak now.** Under the Act, the reasons you give in your payment response are the reasons you can rely on. If you leave a deduction out, you generally cannot raise it later in adjudication. Assess the claim fully the first time.

## Step 1: Log the claim when it arrives

Record the date the claim was served, the claim number, the subcontract and the period it covers. Work out the payment response due date straight away from the subcontract and the Act, and put it in the diary of whoever will sign the response. Most lost disputes start with a response that was simply late.

Check the claim is complete: it should identify the work and period, show the amount claimed and how it is calculated, and refer to the subcontract.

## Step 2: Check the work done

This is the core of the assessment. Compare each line of the claim with the evidence of what was actually done:

- **Measured work**: quantities against site measurements, drawings marked up by your site team, or the agreed method in the subcontract
- **Lump sum items**: percentage complete against the agreed breakdown, checked on site rather than accepted from the claim
- **Variations**: only instructed variations, at the agreed or assessed value, and only to the extent the work is done
- **Materials on site**: only if the subcontract allows payment for them, and only for materials actually on site and protected

Ask your site team to sign off the quantities they agree with. Where you value an item lower than claimed, record the reason against that line, because you will need it in the response.

## Step 3: Apply retention

Deduct retention at the rate and up to the limit in the **subcontract**, not the main contract. These are often different. Show cumulative retention held to date and the amount released, if any. For how retention works and is released, see [retention sum in Singapore](/resources/retention-sum-singapore).

## Step 4: Deduct previous payments

Progress claims are usually cumulative: the gross value of work done to date, less retention, less what has already been certified or paid. Check the subcontractor's figure for previous payments against your own records. Differences here are common and easy to settle early.

## Step 5: Back-charges and other deductions

Back-charges are costs you incurred because of the subcontractor, such as rectifying defective work, clearing their debris, or materials and plant you supplied to them. Deduct them only where:

- the subcontract gives you the right to,
- you can show the cost with evidence (records, photos, invoices, instructions given), and
- the deduction and its reasons are written into the payment response.

Unsupported or unexplained back-charges invite a dispute that you are likely to lose. If a deduction is for damage, loss and expense, check first whether it is supported by a written agreement or a certificate issued under the subcontract, because the SOP Act limits what an adjudicator will consider.

## Step 6: Issue the payment response

The payment response is your formal answer. Based on the Act and BCA's guidance, it should:

1. Be in writing and addressed to the subcontractor
2. Identify the payment claim it answers
3. State the response amount, or nil
4. Give every reason where the response amount is lower than the claimed amount, including each amount withheld
5. Break down the items and show the calculations

Send it by the due date, by a method the subcontract allows, and keep proof of service. A short assessment sheet that mirrors the claim line by line, with your value, the difference and the reason, makes the response easy to read and easy to defend.

| Line | Claimed | Assessed | Difference | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sprinkler pipework, Level 3 | 48,000 | 42,000 | 6,000 | 85% installed at site check on 20 Oct, not 100% |
| VO 004 additional heads | 6,500 | 6,500 | 0 | Instructed and complete |
| Retention at 5% | | (2,425) | | Subcontract clause on retention |
| Back-charge: rectification of damaged ceiling | | (1,200) | | Rectification by others, evidence attached |

*Example only; figures in SGD before GST.*

## Step 7: Pay by the due date

Once the response is issued, pay the response amount by the payment due date in the subcontract, again within the Act's limits. If the subcontractor accepts your response, it would normally then issue its tax invoice. Under IRAS guidance for the construction sector, a contractor issues the tax invoice once its work is certified, and you need a valid tax invoice addressed to your company before you claim the GST as input tax.

## Keep the records together

For each claim, keep the claim, your assessment sheet, site measurements and photos, the payment response and proof of service, the tax invoice and the payment record in one place. IRAS requires GST-registered businesses to keep invoices and supporting records for at least 5 years, and the same file is your evidence if the claim goes to adjudication.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing the response date.** Without a timely response, the subcontractor can go to adjudication and your reasons may not be heard.
- **Withholding without reasons.** A lower figure with no explanation is hard to defend.
- **Using the main contract's retention rate.** Apply what the subcontract says.
- **Back-charges with no evidence.** Record them when they happen, not when the claim arrives.
- **Relying on the client's certificate.** Your client certifying less than you claimed is not, by itself, a reason to pay your subcontractor less.

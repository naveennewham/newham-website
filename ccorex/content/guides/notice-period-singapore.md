In Singapore the **notice period is whatever the employment contract says**, and it must be the same for the employer and the employee. If the contract says nothing, MOM sets a **minimum by length of service: 1 day, 1 week, 2 weeks or 4 weeks**. Either side can end the contract without serving notice by **paying salary in lieu of notice**.

This guide covers the minimum notice, how notice is counted, offsetting leave, salary in lieu and when the final salary is due. Figures are as of October 2026, from MOM's termination pages.

## Minimum notice when the contract is silent

| Length of service | Minimum notice |
|---|---|
| Less than 26 weeks | 1 day |
| 26 weeks to less than 2 years | 1 week |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 2 weeks |
| 5 years or more | 4 weeks |

If the contract does set a notice period, that period applies to both sides. Notice can be waived by mutual agreement, which should be put in writing.

## How notice is given and counted

- **In writing.** Neither side has to give a reason. The employee should keep the employer's acknowledgement.
- **Calendar days.** The notice period includes the day notice is given, plus public holidays, rest days and non-working days.
- **Sick leave counts.** For employees covered by the Employment Act, paid or unpaid sick leave during notice counts towards the notice period.
- **Reservist training does not count.** It extends the notice period by the same length of time.
- **Unpaid leave.** It extends the notice period only if the employee agrees.

## Salary in lieu of notice

Instead of serving notice, either party can pay the salary the employee would have earned over the notice period. As of October 2026, MOM says:

| | CPF payable? |
|---|---|
| Salary earned while serving notice | Yes |
| Salary in lieu of notice | No |

Fixed-term and term-contract employees can also resign before the term ends if they serve the contract's notice or pay salary in lieu.

## Offsetting notice with annual leave

| | Offsetting notice with leave | Taking approved leave during notice |
|---|---|---|
| Needs employer agreement | Yes | Yes (leave approval) |
| Last day of employment | Moves earlier | Stays the same |
| Pay | Up to the earlier last day; the offset leave is not paid | The full notice period |
| Can start a new job | Right after the earlier last day | After the original last day |

Unused annual leave is paid out when employment ends, except when the employee is dismissed for misconduct, where it is forfeited.

## When the final salary is due

| Situation | Final salary due |
|---|---|
| Employee resigns and serves notice | On the last day of employment |
| Employee resigns without serving notice | Within 7 days of the last day |
| Employer ends the contract | On the last day, or within 3 working days if that is not possible |
| Dismissal for misconduct | On the last day, or within 3 working days of dismissal if that is not possible |

For foreign employees who are leaving, MOM says the employer must withhold money due, including salary and leave pay, for up to 30 days for tax clearance.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting only working days.** Notice runs in calendar days, including weekends and public holidays.
- **Extending notice for sick leave.** For Employment Act employees, sick leave during notice counts towards it.
- **Paying CPF on salary in lieu.** CPF is not payable on salary in lieu of notice.
- **Treating offset leave as paid leave.** When leave is used to bring the last day forward, the offset days are not paid.
- **Paying the final salary late.** The deadline depends on how employment ended; check the table above.

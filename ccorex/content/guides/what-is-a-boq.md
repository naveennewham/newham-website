A BOQ (bill of quantities) is an itemised list of the work and materials needed to build a project, with a description, unit and quantity for each item. Contractors add a rate and amount to each line, so every tenderer prices the same list and the client can compare tenders on the same basis. After award, the priced BOQ becomes the reference for valuing progress, pricing variations and setting the contractor's budget.

This page explains the bill of quantities meaning in practical terms, what a typical BOQ format looks like, the main types of BOQ, and how it differs from related documents.

## Bill of quantities meaning

The name describes what it is: a "bill", or list, of "quantities". Each line identifies one piece of work, such as a length of pipe, a number of sprinkler heads or one air handling unit, described clearly enough that a contractor can price it without guessing.

A BOQ does not tell the contractor how to build. The drawings and specification do that. The BOQ translates the design into measurable, priceable items.

BOQs are typically prepared to a standard method of measurement (SMM), a published set of rules on how each kind of work is measured and described. Using a common method means "pipework measured in linear metres, fittings measured separately" means the same thing to everyone. In Singapore, the tender documents usually state which method applies, and consultant and client formats vary from project to project, so always read the preambles.

## What a BOQ is used for

| Use | Who relies on it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Tender pricing | Contractors | Gives every tenderer the same list to price |
| Tender comparison | Client and consultant | Allows line-by-line comparison of tenders |
| Contract document | Both parties | Records the agreed rates as part of the contract |
| Progress valuation | Contractor and consultant | Work done is measured and valued against BOQ items |
| Variations | Both parties | BOQ rates are a starting point for pricing changes |
| Cost control | Contractor | Forms the base for the project budget and purchasing |

## BOQ format: columns

Most BOQs use the same core columns, though layouts differ by consultant.

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Item no. | Unique reference for the line |
| Description | What is supplied and installed, with size, material and specification |
| Unit | Unit of measurement, such as m, m², nr, set, lot or item |
| Quantity | The measured quantity |
| Rate | Price per unit, filled in by the contractor |
| Amount | Quantity × rate |

MEP contractors often keep a more detailed version internally, splitting the rate into material and installation so each can be checked and marked up separately, then carry the combined rate onto the client's form. ## BOQ format: sections

A complete BOQ is usually divided into sections. Names vary, but the content is broadly similar.

### Preliminaries

Items that relate to the project as a whole rather than a specific piece of work: site management, insurances, temporary facilities, programme requirements, and general obligations set out in the contract.

### Preambles

Notes that explain how the BOQ should be read and priced: the method of measurement used, what each rate is deemed to include, and workmanship or material standards that apply across items.

### Measured work

The main body of the BOQ, broken down by trade or system and often by building and floor. For MEP this covers pipework, ductwork, cabling, equipment, devices, valves, insulation and supports.

### Provisional sums and prime cost sums

- **Provisional sums** are allowances for work that cannot yet be fully defined. They are adjusted when the actual work is known.
- **Prime cost (PC) sums** are allowances for items to be supplied by a nominated supplier or subcontractor. The contractor usually prices its own attendance and profit separately.

Price these exactly as the tender instructs.

### Daywork

Rates or percentages for work that will be paid on time and materials rather than measured quantities, if the contract allows it.

### Summary

The roll-up of all sections into a tender total. In Singapore the summary normally shows the total before GST and GST at the prevailing rate separately, unless the tender says otherwise.

## Types of BOQ

The term "BOQ" covers several different documents. Knowing which one you have changes how you price it.

### Firm (detailed) BOQ

Prepared from a complete or near-complete design. Quantities are expected to be accurate, and the contract sum is based on them. Errors in quantities may be corrected under the contract terms.

### Approximate or provisional BOQ

Prepared when the design is not finished. Quantities are estimates and the work is remeasured as it is built, with payment based on actual quantities at the tendered rates.

### Elemental BOQ

Items grouped by building element or system (for example, by sprinkler system, chilled water system or fire alarm system) rather than by trade. Useful for cost planning and comparing projects.

### Trade BOQ

Items grouped by trade, which suits subcontract packaging and is common in MEP work.

### Contractor's BOQ

On Design & Build tenders, the contractor measures the work from its own design development and prepares the BOQ. The format is the contractor's, unless the client provides a pricing schedule to fill in.

### Priced and unpriced BOQ

An unpriced BOQ is the version issued for tender. A priced BOQ is the same document with the contractor's rates and amounts filled in.

## BOQ vs related documents

- **BOQ vs schedule of rates.** A schedule of rates lists items and asks for rates, but has no quantities or only indicative ones. It is used where the amount of work is unknown, such as term maintenance contracts.
- **BOQ vs estimate.** The estimate is the contractor's internal calculation of cost and price. It sits behind the BOQ and often holds more detail.
- **BOQ vs material take-off.** A material take-off (MTO) is the measured list of materials from the drawings. It feeds the BOQ but is usually more detailed. See [quantity takeoff methods](/resources/quantity-takeoff-methods).
- **BOQ vs specification.** The specification describes standards and performance. The BOQ describes and quantifies items for pricing.

## Checklist: reading a BOQ you have been sent

- [ ] Preambles read, including the method of measurement
- [ ] Every section matched to drawings and specification
- [ ] Quantities spot-checked against drawings
- [ ] Missing items identified and qualified in writing
- [ ] Units checked for consistency within each section
- [ ] What each rate is deemed to include understood
- [ ] Provisional and PC sums identified and treated as instructed
- [ ] Summary layout and GST presentation confirmed

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating a BOQ as complete.** Billed quantities can be wrong and items can be missing. Check before you price.
2. **Ignoring the preambles.** They define what your rates include. Skipping them leads to under-pricing.
3. **Changing the client's format.** Adding or renumbering items makes your tender hard to compare.
4. **Confusing provisional and PC sums.** They are adjusted differently and attract different attendance and profit treatment.
5. **Losing the BOQ after award.** The priced BOQ is the basis for claims and variations. Keep the final submitted version where the project team can find it.
